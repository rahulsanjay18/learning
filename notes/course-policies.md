# Course policies

These apply to every course in every major. Each syllabus links here instead of repeating them.

## How we meet
Each day has 10 minutes of review followed by two 25-minute blocks. Each syllabus lists the days its course meets. Do the whole
day's work before going deeper into any one course. After that, extra time is yours to spend however you like.

**Office hours** are any session. Ask in chat, or write your question in a lesson's note box and I'll answer it next time.

## Readings
Most lessons start with a short reading, usually 6 to 10 pages, along with three questions to keep in mind as you read. Do the
reading before the lesson if you can. Every lesson is written so you can still follow it if you didn't.

Page numbers refer to the edition named in the syllabus. If your copy is different, tell me and I'll fix the references.

## Missed days and late work
There is no penalty for missing a day. The schedule just moves back. If you're away for a week or more, we'll start the next
session with a short review before going on.

The engineering courses have one firm rule. Each lesson ends with a real task from your project backlog, and the next lesson in
that track waits until you finish the task or tell me why you're skipping it.

## Grading
Every lesson has two or three objectives. I mark each one **got it** or **not yet**. If it's "not yet", the next lesson
starts with a short re-teach and checks it again. Written answers are graded against a rubric, and you'll see the rubric with
your grade.

Most courses start with a pretest. It never counts against you. If you already know a section, you skip it. If you pass the
whole pretest, the course counts as complete. When you don't know something, say "I don't know". That's the answer that helps.

Each syllabus gives grade weights the way a normal course would. The letter grade is there so you can see where you stand.
Nothing else depends on it.

| Grade | What it means |
|---|---|
| A | 90% or more of the course's objectives marked "got it" on the final check |
| B | 80–89% |
| C | 70–79% |
| Not yet | under 70%: we go back over the missing objectives before closing the course |

Book exercises are optional and don't count toward the grade. The exceptions are courses where the work you produce is the
point, such as a certification exam, a design document or a capstone. The syllabus says so when that applies.

## Honesty
Write your own answers. On homework and projects, look up anything you want and note what you used. On pretests and checks,
answer from memory. If you guessed and got it right, say so and I'll count it as not known. That way the course teaches you that
material instead of skipping it.

## Accommodations
These are part of every course:
- Short blocks, with one clear thing to learn in each lesson.
- Review comes first each day because it's the easiest thing to start.
- Anything you miss comes back in later reviews, spaced out over days.
- When a lesson uses an earlier idea, it restates that idea first.
- At the start of each session you'll see what's due and how long it's been open.

If something here doesn't work for you, tell me and we'll change it.

## Changing a course
You can reorder topics, skip something, swap a book or follow a tangent at any time. I'll make small changes as they come up
and note them. For bigger changes, I'll suggest the change first and you decide. New interests go on the ideas list rather than
into a course that's already running.
