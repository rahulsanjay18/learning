# Is learning on a computer (no handwritten notes) effective? (2026-10-06)

## Short answer
**Mostly yes, for how this platform works.** What you *do* with material matters far more than pen vs. keyboard. Retrieving it
(quizzes, typed answers from memory) and spacing it out are the two best-supported techniques there are, and both are built into
the platform. The real risks of screens are **distraction** (relevant with ADHD) and **slightly worse comprehension when reading
dense text on a screen**, not the lack of handwritten notes.

## The evidence
1. **Typing vs. handwriting notes: no clear difference.** The famous 2014 "pen is mightier than the keyboard" result (laptop
   note-takers wrote verbatim and did worse on conceptual questions) did not hold up: a direct replication with 145 students plus a
   mini meta-analysis of eight studies found no significant difference in learning between typed and handwritten notes [1].
2. **The method matters most.** A major review rated **practice testing** and **distributed (spaced) practice** as the only two
   "high utility" techniques; **rereading, highlighting and summarization** were rated low [2]. This platform is built on the
   high-utility pair: every lesson quizzes you, free responses make you produce answers, and the review deck spaces them.
   Note-taking that mostly copies the text is close to rereading: low value on paper or on screen.
3. **Screen reading has a small cost.** A meta-analysis of 54 studies (171,055 readers) found paper beats screens for
   comprehension of informational text (g ≈ −0.21, a small effect). The gap grows under time pressure and is absent for
   narrative text [3]. Your K&R and Casella & Berger readings are informational, so this applies.
4. **Distraction is the big one.** Students multitasking on a laptop during a lecture scored 11% lower, and people who could see
   a multitasking neighbour scored 17% lower [4]. With inattentive ADHD, other tabs and notifications are the main threat to
   on-screen learning.

## What to do (small changes, no new system)
- **Keep the reading-guide boxes and free responses typed, in your own words.** That's retrieval and self-explanation, the useful
  part of note-taking. Don't paste from the reading.
- **Paper where it clearly helps:** derivations, proofs and calculations (you already do this), and optionally a print or e-ink
  reader for long, dense readings. Self-paced reading shrinks the screen gap [3], so don't rush readings.
- **One tab, full screen, notifications off** during a block. Close the chat with me while you work and come back after.
- **Don't keep a separate notes file** unless it helps you think. The lessons, your answers (on the progress server), the
  `QUESTIONS.md` replies and the review deck already are your notes, and they come back on schedule.
