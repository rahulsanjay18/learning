Checks first

Before you move, list every check your opponent would have. About 25 minutes.

1 · Warm-up: count the square

Two new positions from lesson 1. Recall the tally from memory: cheapest piece recaptures first, and either side may stop.

Two attackers (knight, queen) against two defenders (knight, queen). Who comes out ahead?

Nxd5 Nxd5: +1, −2. Continuing with Qxd5 Qxd5 only makes it worse for White (+1, then −8), so White stops at −2. This is the same shape as lesson 1's worked example: a defended pawn, captured by a piece worth more.

Bishop takes knight, and the knight is defended by a pawn. Who comes out ahead?

Bxd5 exd5 Rxd5: +3, 0, +1. The first trade is even (bishop for knight). Then the recapturing pawn is left undefended, and the rook takes it: +1.

2 · The move that lost your game

You were winning. Black had just played …exf3, and you recaptured with 31. gxf3. The material count says that's fine: it's just a pawn trade. Now imagine the move made and ask one question first: what checks would Black have?

Black's check after 31. gxf3Can White meet it?
…Qe3+Only by moving the king. Then the queen keeps checking (…Qd2+), and Black mates in 5 whatever you do
…Qe1+Rxe1, fine
…Rd1+Rxd1, fine

Three checks, and one of them is a forced mate in 5. Before gxf3, your g-pawn sat next to your king. The capture pulled it away, and your king had nowhere safe to hide from the queen's checks along the e3–g1 diagonal and the second rank.

Compare 31. Re7!, the engine's move: it attacks the queen, and Black's only checks are …Rd1+ (Rxd1) and …f2+ (Kxf2). Both are easy to meet, and White stays winning.3

The routine (Heisman: checks, captures, threats1). After choosing a move, before playing it:

  1. Picture the move made.
  2. List every check your opponent would have. For each one, do you have a good answer?
  3. Then every capture: run the lesson 1 tally.
  4. Then every threat.

Why checks first? A check must be answered at once, so it takes away your choices. If you skip this step and just hope there's an answer, that's what Heisman calls Hope Chess.1

Today we drill step 2 only. One step at a time is how a routine becomes automatic.

3 · Practice A: count the checks

White plays the arrowed move. How many different checks would Black then have? Count every checking move, including captures that give check. Picture the move made; the board shows the position before it.

After Qd7, how many checks does Black have?

2: …Re1+ (down the open e-file) and …Qxh2+ (the queen's long diagonal from b8 to h2). Both can be met (Rxe1, Kxh2), so Qd7 is safe. Counting them is still the habit.

After Re7, how many checks does Black have?

2: …Rd1+ and the pawn check …f2+. Rxd1 and Kxf2 answer them. This is the safe alternative to gxf3.

Black's queen is hanging, and White takes it. After Qxb2, how many checks does Black have?

1: …Rxe1, and it's checkmate. The queen on d2 was the only defender of e1, and capturing on b2 pulled it away. The lesson 1 tally says “+9”, but the check says “game over”. That's why checks come before captures. (Checks first applies to your moves too: White had Rxe8, mate, all along.)

A king walk instead. After Kf1, how many checks does Black have?

6: …Qb5+, …Qe2+, …Qe1+, …Rd1+, …Bd3+ and …fxg2+. After …Qe2+ Kg1 Qxg2# it's mate in 2. A king standing next to open lines invites checks. The more checks your opponent has, the more likely one of them is deadly.

4 · Practice B: safe or lost?

List Black's checks after White's move, then decide. Safe = White doesn't lose material or get mated. Lost = it does. Every verdict was checked with Stockfish 164.

White grabs a pawn on the far side of the board. Safe or lost?

Safe. Black's only check is …Qe1+, and Rxe1 wins the queen. Having a check isn't the same as having a good check; what matters is whether you can meet it. (White's best was Rb8+, mating in 2: look at your own checks first, too.)

The bishop on c5 is undefended. White's rook takes it. Safe or lost?

Lost. …Ra1+ Rc1 Rxc1+ Qd1 Rxd1#, mate in 3. The rook on c1 was guarding your back rank; it left, so Black's rook gets in. The tally says “+3”, and the check says it's mate.

Same position, a different move. Safe or lost?

Safe. Black's only check is …Bxf2+, and Kxf2 just wins the bishop. The rook stays home, so the back rank stays guarded. Rxc5 can wait a move.

5 · Take it to a real game

Play one more untimed bot game (Sven again is fine, with hints and takebacks off). One new rule:

Before every capture, and before every move of a pawn in front of your king: picture the move made, and name every check your opponent would have.

Keep using lesson 1's question too. Then paste the PGN. I'll look for any move where an opponent's check came as a surprise.
