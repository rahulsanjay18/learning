## guard-list

::: exact guard-list-v4 answer="f3"
You have a knight on e5 and pawns on f3, f4 and e3. You're about to move the knight to c4. Step 1: which of those three pawns was the
knight guarding? Type the square.
--- explain
**f3**. A knight on e5 jumps to c4, c6, d3, d7, f3, f7, g4 and g6, so of the three it guards only f3. (From your 6 October game:
after 26…Nc4+ nothing guarded the f3 pawn, and White's king later took it.)
:::

## hang-after

::: free hang-after-v5
In one or two sentences: why can a move be "safe" when you play it (nothing hangs) and still lose material two moves later? Use the
idea of a defender and a closed line.
--- rubric
Got it: the move removes a defender (the moved piece stops guarding something), and an attacker's line is still closed by a piece that
will move, often in a trade; once the line opens, the target has more attackers than defenders. Any example is fine (13. e4 and d4).
:::
