Course description

A Level II course on performance engineering in C and C++: how memory really works (Drepper's paper), how to optimize for modern CPUs (Agner Fog's manuals), and how to measure (perf, cachegrind, Google Benchmark). The final project is a measured, explained speedup of your engine.

Learning objectives

By the end of this course you can:

  1. Explain DRAM, caches, the TLB and NUMA, and their costs in cycles.
  2. Write cache-friendly and prefetch-friendly data structures.
  3. Use SIMD where it helps.
  4. Measure with perf, cachegrind and microbenchmarks, and avoid benchmarking mistakes.
  5. Explain a speedup in terms of the hardware.

Texts

  • Required (free): Ulrich Drepper, What Every Programmer Should Know About Memory (2007): Sections 2 "Commodity Hardware Today", 3 "CPU Caches", 4 "Virtual Memory", 5 "NUMA Support", 6 "What Programmers Can Do", 7 "Memory Performance Tools".
  • Required (free): Agner Fog, Optimizing Software in C++.
  • Recommended (in your library): CS:APP Chapters 5–6, already read in CP120.
