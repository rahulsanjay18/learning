## Course description
What C code turns into on the machine, from Bryant and O'Hallaron's *Computer Systems: A Programmer's Perspective*: data
representation, x86-64 assembly, optimizing for performance, the memory hierarchy and caches, linking, and virtual memory. Your
computer engineering degree covered much of this, so the pretest will probably cut it in half. The rep is a measured speedup in
your engine's hot path.

## Learning objectives
By the end of this course you can:
1. Explain two's complement, floating-point representation and their edge cases.
2. Read the x86-64 assembly your C compiles to, including procedure calls and the stack.
3. Find and remove performance bottlenecks with profiling, loop transformations and attention to the critical path.
4. Explain caches, locality and the memory hierarchy, and restructure code to use the cache well.
5. Explain how static and dynamic linking work, and debug a link error.
6. Explain virtual memory, page tables and the TLB, and their performance effects.

## Texts
- **Required (in your library):** Bryant and O'Hallaron, *Computer Systems: A Programmer's Perspective*, 3rd ed. (2015): Chapter 2 "Representing and Manipulating Information", Chapter 3 "Machine-Level Representation of Programs", Chapter 5 "Optimizing Program Performance", Chapter 6 "The Memory Hierarchy", Chapter 7 "Linking", Chapter 9 "Virtual Memory".
- **Recommended (in your library):** van der Linden, *Expert C Programming*, the chapters on linking and the runtime.
- **Tool:** Compiler Explorer (godbolt.org), to read the assembly from your own code.
