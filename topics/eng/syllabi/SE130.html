Course description

Software design at the level of modules and interfaces, from John Ousterhout's A Philosophy of Software Design. The book argues that the main enemy is complexity, and that deep modules (simple interfaces over substantial functionality) are the main defense. Staff engineers set code-level standards for their group, so the course ends with a short design guide for one of your codebases.

Learning objectives

By the end of this course you can:

  1. Name the symptoms of complexity (change amplification, cognitive load, unknown unknowns) in real code.
  2. Tell deep modules from shallow ones and redesign a shallow one.
  3. Find information leakage and fix it.
  4. Reduce error-handling complexity by defining errors out of existence.
  5. Use "design it twice" on a real interface.
  6. Write a short design guide for a codebase.

Texts

  • Required (in your library): Ousterhout, A Philosophy of Software Design (1st ed., 2018): "The Nature of Complexity", "Working Code Isn't Enough", "Modules Should Be Deep", "Information Hiding (and Leakage)", "General-Purpose Modules are Deeper", "Pull Complexity Downwards", "Define Errors Out of Existence", "Design it Twice".
  • Recommended (in your library): Martin, Clean Architecture, the parts on boundaries and the dependency rule.
  • Not used: Martin, Clean Code. It works at the level of individual lines, which is below the scope of this course.
