## Course description
Scott Meyers's *Effective Modern C++*: 42 items on using C++11 and C++14 well, three or four items a lesson. Type deduction, auto,
the modern features (braced initialization, nullptr, enum class, override, noexcept, constexpr), smart pointers, rvalue references
and move semantics, perfect forwarding, lambdas, and the concurrency API. For the classic idioms these items assume (RAII, resource
management), we read the matching items in *Effective C++*.

## Learning objectives
By the end of this course you can:
1. Predict how template and auto type deduction work, including the edge cases.
2. Choose among unique_ptr, shared_ptr and weak_ptr, and use make functions and the pimpl idiom correctly.
3. Explain std::move and std::forward, universal references, and when move semantics don't help.
4. Write lambdas with correct captures, including init capture.
5. Use std::async, futures and std::atomic correctly, and know where they surprise people.
6. Cross-reference each item with the C++ Core Guidelines.

## Texts
- **Required (in your library):** Scott Meyers, *Effective Modern C++* (O'Reilly, 2014): Chapter 1 "Deducing Types" (Items 1–4), Chapter 2 "auto" (5–6), Chapter 3 "Moving to Modern C++" (7–17), Chapter 4 "Smart Pointers" (18–22), Chapter 5 "Rvalue References, Move Semantics, and Perfect Forwarding" (23–30), Chapter 6 "Lambda Expressions" (31–34), Chapter 7 "The Concurrency API" (35–40), Chapter 8 "Tweaks" (41–42).
- **Recommended (in your library):** Meyers, *Effective C++*, 3rd ed., the items on resource management, construction and destruction.
- **Reference:** the C++ Core Guidelines (isocpp.github.io).
