Course description

Technical strategy: deciding where a group should go and writing it down so others can follow it. We use Rumelt's definition of a strategy (a diagnosis, a guiding policy, and coherent actions) and apply it with Larson's and Reilly's methods for engineering strategy and vision. The deliverable is a strategy document for a real area: your team at work, the 3D chess platform, or this learning platform.

Learning objectives

By the end of this course you can:

  1. Tell a strategy from a goal or a list of wishes, using Rumelt's kernel.
  2. Write an engineering strategy by synthesizing several design decisions.
  3. Write a technical vision for two to three years out.
  4. Use team structure to make a strategy workable.
  5. Get a strategy adopted: sponsors, review, and keeping it current.

Texts

  • Required (to buy): Richard Rumelt, Good Strategy/Bad Strategy (2011), Chapters 1–5.
  • Required (in your library): Larson, Staff Engineer, "Writing engineering strategy". Larson, An Elegant Puzzle, Chapter 3 "Tools", the sections on strategies and visions.
  • Required (in your library): Reilly, The Staff Engineer's Path, Ch. 3 "Creating the Big Picture" (reread for the strategy parts).
  • Optional (to buy): Skelton and Pais, Team Topologies (2019), Part I.
