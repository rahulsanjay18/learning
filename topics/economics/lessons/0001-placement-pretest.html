Microeconomics

What is the opportunity cost of a choice?

"Opportunity cost is the value of the next best alternative" (OpenStax). Time and forgone options count, not just money.

The price of coffee rises sharply. For tea, a substitute, what happens?

Some coffee drinkers switch, so at every tea price more tea is wanted: demand for tea shifts right.

Supply of a good increases (say, a cheaper way to make it) while demand stays the same. At the new equilibrium…

Supply shifts right along an unchanged demand curve: lower price, higher quantity.

A 10% price increase cuts quantity demanded by 20%. What is the (absolute) price elasticity of demand?

|% change in quantity| ÷ |% change in price| = 20 ÷ 10 = 2: elastic, since it's above 1.

A rent-control law sets the maximum rent below the market equilibrium. What results?

A binding price ceiling: quantity demanded exceeds quantity supplied, a shortage; fewer apartments get rented, not more.

A factory's pollution harms nearby residents who aren't part of the transaction. Economists call this a…

Negative externality: a cost borne by third parties, so the market produces more than is socially efficient.

Compared with a perfectly competitive industry, a monopoly typically…

A monopoly sells a lower quantity at a higher price than a competitive market would.

Macroeconomics

Is each one counted (on its own) in this year's GDP?

GDP counts final goods and services produced this year. Used goods were counted when new; stocks are financial assets, not production; steel is an intermediate good already inside the car's value (avoids double counting); government purchases count.

Nominal GDP grows 5% and inflation is 3%. Approximately what is real GDP growth, in percent?

Real growth ≈ nominal growth − inflation = 5 − 3 = 2%.

6 million people are unemployed and 94 million employed. What is the unemployment rate, in percent?

Unemployed ÷ labor force = 6 ÷ (6 + 94) = 6%. People not looking for work aren't in the labor force at all.

To bring down high inflation, a central bank typically…

Contractionary monetary policy raises interest rates, discouraging borrowing and spending. Cutting taxes is fiscal policy, and expansionary.

Two countries can both gain from trade even if one is better at producing everything. The idea that explains this is…

Comparative advantage: each specializes where its opportunity cost is lowest.

A government cuts taxes and raises spending to fight a recession. That's…

Fiscal policy (government taxing and spending). Monetary policy is the central bank's interest rates and money supply.
