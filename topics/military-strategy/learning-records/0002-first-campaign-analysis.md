# First campaign analysis: strong on means, conflates offense with annihilation

The learner's first ends/ways/means breakdown (First Punic War, see [[analyses/2026-10-03-first-punic-war.md]]) shows they can
apply the frame and are strongest on **means**: they spotted the sea vs. land asymmetry and, without prompting, the strategic
weight of Carthage's mercenary army (Polybius 6.52 makes the same argument).

**Misconceptions to correct:**
- "Aggressively seizing territory" was labeled *annihilation*. Annihilation means destroying forces in one or two battles; a 24-year war of sieges is in the slow row.
- Ends and ways were treated as fixed for the whole war. Rome's end escalated after Agrigentum (Polybius 1.20) and its way changed at least four times.
- Capacity vs. will at the *end* of a war: Carthage's will held but its capacity (supply by sea) broke (1.62).

**Implications**: lesson 2 should teach **levels and phases**: a battle of annihilation nested in a campaign nested in an
attrition/exhaustion war (Echevarria Ch. 1). Use the First Punic War as the running case, since the learner now owns it.
Revisit "the bet" (the opponent model); the learner skipped it.
