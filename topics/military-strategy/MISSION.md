# Mission: Military Strategy

## Why
Read military history and current conflicts with a sharper eye: understand *why* campaigns and wars
succeeded or failed, not just what happened. Bring that thinking to strategy games and wargames, and
connect classic strategy to my AI work (game theory, adversarial search, multi-agent decision-making).

## Success looks like
- I can take a real war or campaign and write a short analysis of each side's ends, ways (strategy type) and means,
  and explain why it worked or failed.
- I can apply a strategy framework (ends-ways-means-risk, capacity vs. will, etc.) to a real decision of my own
  or to a wargame or strategy game, and defend the choice.
- I can explain how tactics, weapons and technology change which strategies are possible.
- I can say where the main theorists (Sun Tzu, Clausewitz, Liddell Hart, Boyd, Schelling) agree and disagree,
  well enough to use them in an analysis.
- Where one exists, I can state a strategic idea in game-theory or AI terms (e.g. deterrence and commitment,
  attrition and Lanchester's laws).

## Constraints
- Theory first; history serves as worked examples.
- Reading-heavy sessions (~40 min), fewer quizzes than chess, but still one idea per lesson with guided reading questions.
- Inattentive ADHD: structure every reading with questions to hold in mind; keep lessons tightly scoped.
- Citations in text and at the end; save explanations as files.

## Out of scope
- Nothing excluded so far. Tactics, weapons, nuclear strategy, insurgency, cyber and modern conflicts are all in scope.
