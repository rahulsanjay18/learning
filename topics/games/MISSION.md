# Mission: Games

## Why
Understand how decisions in strategy games work (what makes a choice interesting, how rules create depth, how balance is
achieved and measured) well enough to **design a complex, balanced strategy game in 3D space**, more interesting to play than 3D chess.

Also wanted for its own sake: **AI applications**: game-playing agents (search, MCTS, self-play learning), used both to play
and to test designs. The **most immediate project is a 3D chess variant**.

## Success looks like
- Explain, for any rule in chess, xiangqi, shogi, Go, backgammon or poker, what decision problem it creates and what it balances.
- Measure a game's balance (first-player advantage, draw rate, dominant strategies) with self-play agents you wrote.
- Prototype a 3D strategy game, playtest it with people, and iterate it from data and feedback.
- Build agents that play your 3D variant well enough to expose first-move advantage, dominant strategies and draw problems.
- Keep its complexity learnable: humans can actually see and plan in the 3D space.

## Constraints
- Half-weight subject (2 blocks a week, inside the 1-hour-a-day floor); chess is the first class and the ongoing lab.
- Strong technical background (math, CS, 5 years in AI): agents and simulations are fair game.

## Out of scope (for now)
- Video-game production (3D engines, art): the focus is the game's rules and decisions. A digital prototype can come late.
