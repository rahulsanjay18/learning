## Course description
Poker, the hidden-information game. You can't see your opponent's cards, so you reason about the range of hands they could hold.
We cover pot odds, equity and expected value; hand ranges; bluffing and why a balanced strategy must bluff; game-theory-optimal
play versus exploiting a weak opponent; and the mental side of the game. The AI lesson covers counterfactual regret minimization,
the algorithm behind the programs that beat professionals.

## Learning objectives
By the end of this course you can:
1. Compute pot odds, equity and the expected value of a call.
2. Describe a player's range and update it from their actions.
3. Explain why a balanced strategy bluffs at a specific frequency, using the indifference principle and minimum defense frequency.
4. Tell game-theory-optimal play from exploitative play, and say when to use each.
5. Explain how counterfactual regret minimization finds equilibrium strategies.
6. Name the main mental mistakes (tilt, results-oriented thinking) and how to manage them.

## Texts
- **Required:** Acevedo, *Modern Poker Theory* (in your library): "Basic Concepts" (positions, ranges, combinatorics), "Key Metrics" (pot odds, equity, expected value, fold equity), and "The Core Concepts" (exploitative strategy, Nash equilibrium, the indifference principle, the clairvoyance toy game, minimum defense frequency).
- **Required:** Tommy Angelo, *Elements of Poker* (in your library), Part I "Universal Elements", on tilt and decisions.
- **Paper:** Brown and Sandholm, "Superhuman AI for multiplayer poker", *Science* 365 (2019).
