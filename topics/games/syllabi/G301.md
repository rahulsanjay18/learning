## Course description
Go, the most different game in the major. There is no king to checkmate and no pieces that move. Stones are placed and stay, and
the game is won by surrounding territory. We start on the 9×9 board and move to 13×13. Then we look at why Go resisted computers
long after chess fell, and how Monte Carlo tree search and AlphaGo changed that. Komi, the points given to the second player, is
the clearest balancing rule in any game you'll study.

## Learning objectives
By the end of this course you can:
1. Play a full game on 9×9 and 13×13 with Japanese or Chinese scoring, including ko.
2. Decide whether a group is alive or dead, and make two eyes.
3. Read ladders and nets and recognize good and bad shapes.
4. Explain sente and gote, and territory versus influence.
5. Explain why alpha-beta search failed for Go and how Monte Carlo tree search works.
6. Explain komi as a balancing device, and what its size says about the first-move advantage.

## Texts
- **Required:** *A Go Guide by a Beginner* (in your library): Chapter 1 "Introduction" (rules, ranks), Chapter 2 "Shapes", Chapter 3 "Let's Play" (opening, middle game, endgame), Chapter 4 "Joseki and Fuseki".
- **Recommended:** Janice Kim and Jeong Soo-hyun, *Learn to Play Go*, Volumes IV–V (in your library).
- **Paper:** Silver et al., "Mastering the game of Go with deep neural networks and tree search", *Nature* 529 (2016).
