## Course description
Small games you can solve completely: tic-tac-toe, Nim and Hex on a small board. They show the ideas behind every bigger game in
a form you can check exactly. You play each one against a perfect solver, which tells you when a move loses and how fast. The
course ends with the first measurement of your 3D variant: its branching factor and game length.

## Learning objectives
By the end of this course you can:
1. Draw a game tree and label positions as wins or losses by working backward (minimax).
2. Solve Nim with the binary XOR rule, and prove why it works.
3. Explain the strategy-stealing argument for why the first player in Hex can't lose, and why the swap rule exists.
4. Explain parity and tempo with examples.
5. Estimate a game's branching factor and length, and what they imply for search.

## Texts
- **Required:** Albert, Nowakowski and Wolfe, *Lessons in Play: An Introduction to Combinatorial Game Theory* (2nd ed., 2019; to buy), the opening chapters on outcome classes and impartial games.
- **Optional (in your library):** Berlekamp, Conway and Guy, *Winning Ways for Your Mathematical Plays*, Volume 2, for more games.
- **In the lessons:** solver boards for tic-tac-toe, Nim and Hex.
