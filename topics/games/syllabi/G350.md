## Course description
An optional course on wargames: games built to model real conflict, from the Prussian army's Kriegsspiel of 1824 to modern
professional simulations. It covers how a wargame models fog of war, logistics and combat, and what such games can and can't
teach the people who play them. It's the most direct link between the strategy side of the major and your own design work.

## Learning objectives
By the end of this course you can:
1. Explain how Kriegsspiel used an umpire, hidden movement and dice, and why the Prussian army adopted it.
2. Describe the main ways wargames model combat (combat results tables, Lanchester's laws) and logistics.
3. Explain what professional wargaming is used for and what it can't do.
4. Build a small wargame model of a historical battle and test it against what happened.

## Texts
- **Required (in your library):** Peter Perla, *The Art of Wargaming* (2012 edition), Part 1 "Perspectives".
- **Required (to buy):** Philip Sabin, *Simulating War: Studying Conflict through Simulation Games* (Bloomsbury, 2012), Part I.
- **Recommended:** von Reisswitz, *Kriegsspiel* (1824; Leeson's English translation); James Dunnigan, *The Complete Wargames Handbook* (free online).
