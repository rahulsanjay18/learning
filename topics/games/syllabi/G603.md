## Course description
A Level II course on the specific problems of a game in three dimensions: cell geometry (cubes or other shapes), adjacency and
movement, board size against complexity, and how players see and think about 3D space. We look at earlier 3D chess variants,
starting with Raumschach (5×5×5, 1907), and at why they stayed niche. The course produces a revised design for your variant and
a plan for playtesting it.

## Learning objectives
By the end of this course you can:
1. Compare cubic and other cell geometries by adjacency, symmetry and how pieces move.
2. Estimate how board size and piece movement in 3D affect branching factor and game length.
3. Explain the visibility and cognitive-load problems of 3D boards and propose interface fixes.
4. Explain why earlier 3D variants didn't catch on.
5. Produce a revised design for your variant and a playtest plan.

## Texts
- **Required:** Wikipedia, "Three-dimensional chess" (Raumschach and later variants), with the sources it cites.
- **Required:** your G102 measurements and G504 self-play data.
- **Recommended (in your library):** Sellers, *Advanced Game Design*, Chapter 12 "Making Your Game Real".
