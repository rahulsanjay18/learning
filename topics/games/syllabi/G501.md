## Course description
A seminar that puts the game families side by side: chess, xiangqi, shogi and Go. It asks how the same strategic ideas (tempo,
material, king safety, initiative, endgame counting) look under different rules. You've been answering "what carries over?" at
the end of each course. This seminar collects those answers and turns them into a short comparative essay. That essay becomes
the starting point for the design courses.

## Learning objectives
By the end of this course you can:
1. Compare how four game families handle material, tempo, safety and the endgame.
2. Explain which rule differences cause which strategic differences.
3. Argue for a general principle of strategy that holds across the families, and name where it breaks.

## Texts
- **Required:** your design notebook and "what carries over" notes from G101–G301.
- **Recommended (to buy):** Elias, Garfield and Gutschera, *Characteristics of Games* (MIT Press, 2012), the opening chapters.
