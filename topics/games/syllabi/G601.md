## Course description
A Level II design course on what makes a strategy game good. Why does Go have so much depth from so few rules? Why do some games
end in draws and others don't? How do branching factor, game length, information and luck shape the decisions players make? We
read Sellers's systems approach to design alongside two classics, and apply all of it to the games you've played and to your
variant.

## Learning objectives
By the end of this course you can:
1. Describe a game as a system of parts, loops and feedback.
2. Explain what makes a decision interesting (meaningful choice, uncertainty, consequence) with examples.
3. Explain how depth can come from simple rules, using Go and Nim as examples.
4. Use branching factor, game length, information and luck as design levers.
5. Critique your variant's design using these tools.

## Texts
- **Required (in your library):** Michael Sellers, *Advanced Game Design: A Systems Approach* (2017), Chapters 1 "Foundations of Systems", 2 "Defining Systems", 3 "Foundations of Games and Game Design", 4 "Interactivity and Fun", 7 "Creating Game Loops", 8 "Defining Game Parts".
- **Required (to buy):** Elias, Garfield and Gutschera, *Characteristics of Games* (MIT Press, 2012). Salen and Zimmerman, *Rules of Play* (MIT Press, 2003), the units on rules and on games as systems.
