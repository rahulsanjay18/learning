## Course description
Shogi, Japanese chess. Its big difference is the drop: a captured piece changes sides and can be dropped back onto the board.
So the material never runs out, the game doesn't simplify, and draws are rare. Pieces also promote when they enter the last three
ranks. We'll learn the game, its main tactical ideas, and why drops make it the most different of the chess family.

## Learning objectives
By the end of this course you can:
1. Play a full game of shogi with all the rules, including drop restrictions (no two unpromoted pawns on a file, no pawn-drop mate).
2. Use drops in attack and defence.
3. Build a basic castle and attack an opponent's castle.
4. Explain how drops change material value, tempo and the endgame compared with chess.
5. Write a design notebook entry on drops.

## Texts
- **Required:** Trevor Leggett, *Japanese Chess: The Game of Shogi* (in your library).
- **In the lessons:** the shogi board with drops and promotion.
