## Course description
A Level II course on game-playing AI, built around your own 3D variant. It follows the history: minimax and alpha-beta, then
expectimax for chance, Monte Carlo tree search, counterfactual regret minimization for hidden information, and reinforcement
learning by self-play (TD-Gammon, AlphaGo, AlphaZero, MuZero). By the end you'll have a rules engine and an MCTS agent for your
variant, and self-play data on its first-move advantage and draw rate.

## Learning objectives
By the end of this course you can:
1. Implement minimax with alpha-beta pruning and explain how move ordering affects its cost.
2. Implement Monte Carlo tree search with UCT and explain the exploration term.
3. Explain counterfactual regret minimization and run it on a small poker game.
4. Explain temporal-difference learning and policy improvement by self-play.
5. Explain AlphaZero's combination of search and a learned network, and MuZero's learned model.
6. Build an agent for your variant and measure first-move advantage and draw rate with confidence intervals.

## Texts
- **Required (in your library):** Russell and Norvig, *Artificial Intelligence: A Modern Approach*, 3rd ed., Chapter 5 "Adversarial Search".
- **Required (free):** Sutton and Barto, *Reinforcement Learning: An Introduction*, 2nd ed. (2018): Chapter 6 "Temporal-Difference Learning", §8.11 "Monte Carlo Tree Search", Chapter 16 "Applications and Case Studies" (TD-Gammon and AlphaGo).
- **Papers:** Knuth and Moore, "An Analysis of Alpha-Beta Pruning" (1975); Kocsis and Szepesvári, "Bandit Based Monte-Carlo Planning" (2006); Zinkevich et al., "Regret Minimization in Games with Incomplete Information" (2007); Silver et al., AlphaZero, *Science* 362 (2018); Schrittwieser et al., MuZero, *Nature* 588 (2020).
