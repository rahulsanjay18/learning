## Course description
An optional reading course on the history of board games: from the Royal Game of Ur and senet through chaturanga (the Indian
ancestor of chess, xiangqi and shogi) to modern games. It pairs well with the Indian History major, since chess began in India.

## Learning objectives
By the end of this course you can:
1. Trace the spread of chaturanga and its descendants across Asia and Europe.
2. Describe the oldest known board games and how their rules were reconstructed.
3. Explain how chess's rules changed in the late fifteenth century (the modern queen and bishop) and why it mattered.

## Texts
- **Required (to buy):** David Parlett, *The Oxford History of Board Games* (Oxford, 1999).
- **Recommended:** H. J. R. Murray, *A History of Chess* (1913), the chapters on India. Irving Finkel (ed.), *Ancient Board Games in Perspective* (British Museum, 2007).
