Course description

The first course of the major, and the lab the later courses come back to. It starts with safety habits (is my piece safe? what did their move do? what did my move stop defending?), then works through the standard tactical patterns in Seirawan and Silman's Winning Chess Tactics, one chapter per lesson, plus the basic king-and-pawn endings. You show you've learned a pattern by using it in your own games, not only in puzzles. Every game you play during the course is reviewed with an engine and tied back to a lesson.

Learning objectives

By the end of this course you can:

  1. Run a safety check before every move: checks, captures and threats for both sides, and what each piece was defending.
  2. Find double attacks, pins, skewers, deflections, decoys and in-between moves in positions and in your own games.
  3. Spot a back-rank weakness on both sides and fix your own in time.
  4. Use batteries on open files and diagonals.
  5. Win king and pawn against king with the opposition, and recognize the drawn cases.
  6. Review one of your own games and name the turning point and the missed tactic.

Texts

  • Required: Seirawan and Silman, Winning Chess Tactics (in your library), Part 1: Chapter 2 "The Double Attack", 3 "The Pin", 4 "The Skewer", 5 "King Tactics and Combinations", 6 "Deflection", 7 "Battery on an Open File or Diagonal", 9 "The Decoy", 12 "Zwischenzug".
  • Required: Seirawan, Winning Chess Endings (in your library), the king-and-pawn chapter.
  • Optional (in your library): Capablanca, Chess Fundamentals. Winning Chess Tactics Part 2 (the great tacticians' games) for reading on rest days.
  • Your own games, reviewed with Stockfish.
