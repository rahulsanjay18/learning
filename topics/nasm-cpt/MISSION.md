# Mission: NASM Certified Personal Trainer (NASM-CPT)

## Why
Pass the NASM-CPT exam so I can (1) train a few clients as a side gig, (2) train myself and friends with
real knowledge instead of gym lore, and (3) have domain credibility if I move into fitness tech. Not a career change.

## Success looks like
- I pass the NASM-CPT exam (scaled score of 70+ on 120 questions in 2 hours).
- Given a new client's goal and assessment results, I can place them in an OPT phase and write a sensible first program.
- I can run and interpret the overhead squat assessment and other core NASM assessments on a real person.
- I know my scope of practice: when to refer out (medical, diet plans, injuries).
- I can tell where NASM's model is a teaching framework vs. where the wider evidence (e.g. RP, Schoenfeld) says more.

## Constraints
- No exam date yet (3+ months out). Steady pace, 20–30 minutes per session.
- Inattentive ADHD: short lessons, one idea at a time, immediate feedback.
- Some lifting experience; not deeply studied.
- Materials: only my library copies (NASM Essentials 4th ed., 2012, which is older than the 7th-edition exam; Mometrix NASM flashcards).
  Haven't bought the NASM course.

## Out of scope (for now)
- Specializations (CES, PES, nutrition coach). Revisit after passing.
- Fitness-tech product ideas. Revisit after passing.
