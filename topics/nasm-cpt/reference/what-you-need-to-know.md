# NASM-CPT: what you need to know (overview)

_Saved 2026-10-02. Sources at the bottom; numbers in [brackets] refer to them._

## The exam
- **120 multiple-choice questions, 2 hours, pass with a scaled score of 70 or better** [2].
- Six domains, weighted by NASM's 2019 job-analysis blueprint [1]:

| # | Domain | Weight |
|---|---|---|
| 5 | Exercise Technique & Training Instruction | 24% |
| 4 | Program Design | 20% |
| 3 | Assessment | 16% |
| 1 | Basic & Applied Sciences and Nutritional Concepts | 15% |
| 2 | Client Relations & Behavioral Coaching | 15% |
| 6 | Professional Development & Responsibility | 10% |

Some prep sites (e.g. PT Pioneer [5]) list *different* weights with the domains reordered. NASM's own PDF [1] is the authority.

## What's in each domain (condensed from the blueprint [1])
1. **Sciences & nutrition**: body systems, energy systems (ATP-PC, glycolytic, oxidative), EPOC, planes of motion, muscle actions,
   force couples, length–tension, stretch-shortening cycle, reciprocal/autogenic inhibition, macros/micros, hydration, label reading, supplements.
2. **Client relations**: rapport, SMART goals, Stages of Change (Transtheoretical Model), coaching and behavior-change strategies, barriers.
3. **Assessment**: PAR-Q and health history, vitals, body composition, static posture, **overhead squat** and other movement
   assessments, kinetic chain checkpoints, performance tests, special populations, when to refer out.
4. **Program design**: periodization, **the OPT model's levels and phases**, GAS, acute variables, flexibility/resistance/cardio/core/
   balance/plyometric/SAQ training, progressions and regressions, overtraining.
5. **Technique**: set-up and cueing for all of the above, spotting, breathing, safety, signs to stop.
6. **Professional**: scope of practice (no diagnosing, counseling or meal plans), ethics, business and sales, emergency protocols.

## The backbone: the OPT model
Program Design + Technique = 44% of the exam, and both are organized by NASM's **Optimum Performance Training (OPT) model** [1][3].

| Level | Phase | Fingerprint / acute variables [3] |
|---|---|---|
| Stabilization | 1 · Stabilization Endurance | 12–20 reps, slow, lighter load, controlled instability |
| Strength | 2 · Strength Endurance | superset: strength exercise → similar stability exercise; 2–4 sets, 8–12 reps each |
| Strength | 3 · Muscular Development / Hypertrophy | 3–6 sets, 6–12 reps, 75–85% 1RM |
| Strength | 4 · Maximal Strength | 4–6 sets, 1–5 reps, 85–100% 1RM |
| Power | 5 · Power | superset: heavy 1–5 reps → light explosive 8–10 reps |

Most clients start in Phase 1 [4]. Clients climb only as far as their goal needs, then cycle (e.g. fat loss: Phases 1 ↔ 2) [6].

## Your materials, honestly
- Your library has the **4th edition** textbook (2012). The exam follows the **7th edition**. The core model is the same, but naming
  and some content changed (e.g. Phase 3's name). Check facts against nasm.org; the 7th ed. is on `library/WANTED.md`.
- The Mometrix flashcards (in your library) are useful for question style but contain errors.

## Study plan (by exam weight)
1. OPT model (lesson 1) → acute variables per phase
2. Overhead squat assessment: compensations → overactive/underactive muscles
3. Human movement science: planes, muscle actions, force couples
4. Stages of Change and coaching; scope of practice
5. Interleave earlier topics as warm-up retrieval every session.
