The OPT ladder

NASM's 5-phase model, which the whole exam hangs on. About 15 minutes.

Why this comes first

The exam has six domains. The two biggest are Exercise Technique (24%) and Program Design (20%)1, and both are organized around NASM's Optimum Performance Training model, or OPT. The blueprint asks you directly about its “levels (such as stabilization, strength, power)” and “phases (such as stabilization endurance, strength endurance, hypertrophy, maximal strength, power)”1. Learn it once and later topics (assessments, core, balance, plyometrics) each slot into a phase.

Today's win: given a client's goal or a workout description, you can name the OPT phase.

1 · The shape: 3 levels, 5 phases

Think of it as a curriculum with a prerequisite graph: 1 → 3 → 1.

Edition note. Your library copy (4th ed., 2012) and the blueprint call Phase 3 “Hypertrophy”. NASM's current page calls it “Muscular Development/Hypertrophy”3. Same phase; know both names.

2 · The fingerprints

Exam questions describe a workout and ask which phase it is. Each phase has one feature that gives it away:

  • Phase 1: high reps (12–20), slow movement, lighter load, and controlled instability (e.g. a single-leg or ball version)3.
  • Phase 2: a superset: a traditional strength exercise (bench press) followed by a similar movement that needs more stabilization; 8–12 reps each, 2–4 sets3.
  • Phase 3: 3–6 sets of 6–12 reps at 75–85% of 1RM3.
  • Phase 4: 1–5 reps at 85–100% of 1RM, 4–6 sets3.
  • Phase 5: a superset: a heavy exercise for 1–5 reps, then a light one moved as explosively as possible for 8–10 reps3.

Notice the two supersets. Both pair a strength exercise with something else, and the partner tells you the phase: a stability partner means Phase 2, an explosive partner means Phase 5.

3 · Moving along the ladder

Clients don't climb all five rungs by default. They go as far as their goal needs, then cycle. A Mometrix example: a fat-loss client works in Phases 1 and 2, roughly four weeks each, then alternates between them; a client after muscle size progresses 1 → 2 → 3 and may cycle through 2, 3 and 44. Power is only for clients who need explosiveness4.

Treat the Mometrix flashcards as a source of exam-style scenarios, not an authority: one of its cards puts the wrong phase in the stabilization level.

Which level holds three phases?

Strength holds Phases 2, 3 and 4. Stabilization and Power hold one each.2

A client wants to lose fat and has no interest in max strength. Where do they start?

Phase 1, then cycle between Phases 1 and 2.4 Almost every new client starts in Phase 1.2

Write down your own last week of lifting. Which phase does each session look like? If it doesn't fit any phase cleanly, which acute variable (reps, sets, load, superset partner) is off? Bring the answer next session: it's our warm-up.
