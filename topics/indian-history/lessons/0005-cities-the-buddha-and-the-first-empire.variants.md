## urb-bud

::: free urb-bud-v3
In two or three sentences: why did a religion like the Buddha's appear in the Ganges cities of the 5th century BC rather than in the
Vedic villages of a few centuries earlier? Use at least one piece of evidence about the cities.
--- rubric
Got it: links the teaching to the new urban society (traders, artisans, kings as audience and patrons; a reaction against the
Brahmin–Kshatriya order and its sacrifices) and cites one piece of urban evidence (coins, weights, fortified capitals, trade pottery,
monasteries in the cities, Bimbisara as patron). No false statement.
:::

::: choice urb-bud-v4
The earliest Buddhist stories describe a flourishing urban society, and archaeology shows the Gangetic cities only beginning in the
6th century BC and flourishing in the 5th.
What does that suggest about the Buddha's dates?
- [x] He probably lived in the 5th century BC, later than the traditional date
- [ ] He probably lived in the 7th century BC, earlier than the traditional date
- [ ] He probably lived in the 6th century BC, exactly at the traditional date
- [ ] He probably lived in the 3rd century BC, in the time of Ashoka
--- hint
If the stories describe cities, the Buddha can't be much older than the cities.
--- explain
The traditional dates (death in 544 BC, or 483 BC) put him in the 6th century; the city evidence fits Bechert's later date, about a
century after, in the 5th century BC.
:::

## maurya-date

::: number maurya-date-v4 answer=15 tolerance=2
Chandragupta Maurya took the throne of Magadha in about 320 BC; Seleukos ceded him the lands east of Kabul in 305 BC. How many years
apart are the two events? (Remember BC years count down.)
--- explain
320 − 305 = 15 years: BC dates count down toward year 1, so the later event has the smaller number.
:::

## corrob

::: free corrob-v2
A Greek ambassador at the Maurya court wrote that the capital was about 21 miles around. Name one reason to doubt him and one way a
historian checked him.
--- rubric
Doubt: an ambassador may exaggerate the court he was sent to, to make himself look important; or his book is lost and we only have
quotations. Check: the tower and gate spacing he gives matches the 54 yards the Arthashastra prescribes for such forts (Schlingloff),
an independent Indian source. Got it = one of each, no false statement.
:::

::: choice corrob-v3
Megasthenes wrote that all land in India belonged to the king. K&R suggest a limit on that observation. Which one?
- [x] It may hold only for the capital and its hinterland, the area he knew well
- [ ] It may hold only for the northwest frontier, the area that Alexander had once ruled
- [ ] It may hold only for the reign of the Nandas, before Chandragupta had seized power
- [ ] It may hold only for the Buddhist monasteries, which the king himself had richly endowed
--- hint
Where did Megasthenes actually live, and how much of the empire could he have seen?
--- explain
K&R: "Perhaps these observations were applicable only to the capital and its immediate hinterland which was the area which
Megasthenes knew well." A witness is good for what he saw.
:::

## mandala

::: choice mandala-v3
In Kautilya's circle of kings, who is the **neutral king** (udasina)?
- [x] A stronger king outside the whole circle who can interfere with any of its members
- [ ] A weaker king inside the whole circle who borders both the conqueror and his enemy
- [ ] A stronger king inside the whole circle who borders both the conqueror and his enemy
- [ ] A weaker king outside the whole circle who must be conquered before any other king
--- hint
Two special kings break the alternating pattern. One borders both sides of a war; the other doesn't belong to the circle at all.
--- explain
The neutral king stands outside the circle and is stronger than its members, so he can help or crush any of them; the king who borders both sides of a war is the middle king (madhyama).
:::

::: free mandala-v4
A board game has six players seated around a ring map; each player borders only the players on either side. Using Kautilya's
rajamandala, predict who player 1 will treat as a natural enemy and who as a natural ally, and say one thing that could break the
prediction.
--- rubric
Enemies: players 2 and 6 (direct neighbours). Allies: players 3 and 5 (neighbours of neighbours). Player 4 (opposite) is the enemy's
ally in both directions, or effectively outside the immediate rivalry. Breaks: a "middle king" strong enough to decide a war between
two others; an outside power (neutral king); a player who is in calamity and invites attack instead of alliance. Got it = enemies and
allies right and one breaker named.
:::

## mach-claim

::: categorize mach-claim-v2
According to K&R, does each point support or weaken the claim "the Arthashastra is just a Machiavellian book of dirty tricks"?
- Detailed instructions for spies, with insight into human weakness > Supports the claim
- Most of the text is about mines, trade, farming and salaries > Weakens the claim
- High taxes are limited because they drive people to the enemy > Weakens the claim
- No moral limits on taking the country's surplus > Supports the claim
--- explain
K&R grant the spies and the "no moral limits", but say the book's focus is economic prosperity through the state, and that political
feasibility (subjects' contentment) limits what the king can take.
:::

::: free mach-claim-v3
Why might the Arthashastra be a better source for the age of the sixteen rival kingdoms than for the Maurya empire itself?
--- rubric
It assumes several small rival kingdoms, each able to win supremacy by following its advice; that matches the period before Magadha
won, not an established empire. It's normative (how a king should act), and parts were added centuries later. Got it = the rival
kingdoms point, no false statement.
:::

## patali-loc

::: exact patali-loc-v2 answer="Patna|patna, bihar"
Pataliputra, the capital of the Nanda and Maurya kings, lies under which modern Indian city?
--- explain
**Patna**, the capital of Bihar, on the south bank of the Ganga. It began as Pataligrama, a village fortified by Magadha's generals.
:::

::: choice patali-loc-v3
On which river did Pataliputra stand?
- [x] The Ganga, some way downriver (east) of Varanasi
- [ ] The Indus, some way downriver (south) of Taxila
- [ ] The Yamuna, some way upriver (north) of Kausambi
- [ ] The Jhelum, upriver (north) of the Hydaspes battle
--- hint
Magadha was the eastern Ganges kingdom.
--- explain
Pataliputra (Patna) stands on the Ganga in Bihar, east of Varanasi and about 380 km east of Kausambi.
:::
