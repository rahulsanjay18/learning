## Overview
This major covers Indian history from the Indus cities to the present Republic. It meets twice a week, and a typical lesson takes
both meetings: one to read a section of the textbook, one for the lesson. The main text is Kulke and Rothermund's *A History of
India* (6th edition), which has eight chapters, one for each era. You read it twice. The first pass (IH100) is a quick survey to
build a timeline in your head. The second pass (IH201–IH206) goes chapter by chapter, adding a more detailed book and primary
sources for each era.

Every unit also takes a claim about Indian history from today's public debate and asks how well the evidence supports it.

## What you'll be able to do
**After Level I:**
- Tell the story of Indian history from the Indus to today, with dates, places, and the causes of the big changes.
- Read a primary source and ask the right questions: who wrote it, when, for whom, why, and whether to trust it.
- Explain where historians disagree (on the Aryan migration, "Indian feudalism", Mughal decline, the colonial drain, Partition) and which side the evidence favors.
- Judge a public claim about Indian history, with sources.
- Write a short argued answer to a historical question.

**After Level II (optional):**
- Explain the main schools of Indian historiography and read any historian with their school in mind.
- Work with primary sources in translation across 2,000 years.
- Write an original piece of research from primary sources.

## How the major is organized
**Required courses (Level I).** IH100 the whole arc (in progress) → IH150 how historians work → IH201 Ancient India → IH202 Early
medieval and medieval → IH203 The Mughals → IH204 Colonial India → IH205 Freedom and Partition → IH206 The Republic.

**Optional courses.** Four breadth courses that follow a theme across eras: IH310 religion and philosophy, IH320 war and the
state (it pairs with the strategy course in the Games major), IH330 economic history, IH340 art, architecture and literature.
Then IH390 sites, museums and a family oral history; IH400 a research essay; IH398 a closing conversation.

**Level II** only starts if you sign up for it: IH510 historiography seminar, IH520 primary sources in translation, IH530 regional
histories, electives IH540 Partition and memory and IH550 Sanskrit or Persian basics, IH596 a research project, and IH598/IH599
the closing conversation and thesis.

**In every unit:** a short reading, a lesson that places events on a timeline and a map, one primary source, a "why did this
happen?" question, and a debate between historians or a public claim to judge. Each unit ends with a 300-word argued answer.

## Order
Required courses run in the order above, one at a time. The breadth courses fit best right after the era they draw on most:
IH310 after IH201, IH320 after IH203, IH330 after IH204, IH340 any time. The oral history (IH390) is worth doing early, while the
relatives you'd interview can still tell you.

## What it takes to finish
- **Level I:** IH100, IH150 and IH201–IH206.
- **Level II:** IH510, IH520 and IH530.

## Books
**In your library:** Kulke and Rothermund, *A History of India*; Very Short Introductions on Buddhism (Keown), Hinduism (Knott),
Indian Philosophy (Hamilton), Gandhi (Parekh), Sikhism (Nesbitt), Islam (Ruthven), the British Empire (Jackson), Decolonization
(Kennedy), Nationalism (Grosby), Postcolonialism (Young); Adamson and Ganeri, *Classical Indian Philosophy*; Dasgupta, *A History of
Indian Philosophy*; Easwaran, *The Upanishads*; Debroy's *Mahabharata* (some volumes); four New Cambridge History of India volumes
(Stein, *Vijayanagara*; Marshall, *Bengal: The British Bridgehead*; Pearson, *The Portuguese in India*; Grewal, *The Sikhs of the
Punjab*), plus *Mughal and Rajput Painting* and *Caste, Society and Politics*; Tirthankar Roy, *The Economic History of India*;
Manto's stories; Sharma, *Hindutva*; Dasgupta, *Awakening Bharat Mata*.

**To buy, in the order you'll need them:**
1. Upinder Singh, *A History of Ancient and Early Medieval India* (2008), for IH201–IH202.
2. Richard Eaton, *India in the Persianate Age, 1000–1765* (2019), for IH202–IH203.
3. John F. Richards, *The Mughal Empire* (New Cambridge History, 1993), for IH203.
4. Sugata Bose and Ayesha Jalal, *Modern South Asia* (4th ed., 2018), for IH204–IH205.
5. Yasmin Khan, *The Great Partition* (2nd ed., 2017), for IH205.
6. Ramachandra Guha, *India After Gandhi* (2nd ed., 2017), for IH206.
7. For the optional courses: Gommans, *Mughal Warfare*; Partha Mitter, *Indian Art*; Butalia, *The Other Side of Silence*; and the Level II readers listed in those syllabi.
