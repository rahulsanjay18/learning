Course description

An optional practice course with two projects. The first is a museum or site visit (in person, or through a museum's online collection), studied the way a historian would study it. The second is an oral-history interview with a relative about family history, especially Partition-era memories if your family has them. Oral history is a real historical method with its own rules, and recordings of people who remember 1947 won't be possible for much longer.

Learning objectives

By the end of this course you can:

  1. Prepare and conduct an oral-history interview: consent, open questions, follow-ups, and recording.
  2. Treat an interview as a source: what memory preserves, what it changes, and how to corroborate it.
  3. Write up a site or museum visit, connecting objects to the history you've learned.

Texts

  • Required: the 1947 Partition Archive's interview guidelines (free online); the Oral History Association's "Principles and Best Practices" (free online).
  • Recommended: Butalia, The Other Side of Silence (to buy), the opening chapter.
