Course description

An optional breadth course on India's economic history from 1707 to the present, using Tirthankar Roy's The Economic History of India, 1857–2010 (4th edition), which is in your library. Roy argues that colonial India's economy was a mix of different regional experiences, not one story of decline. You'll test that argument against the "drain" view you met in IH204. The course also draws on your economics background: growth accounting, trade, and the role of the state.

Learning objectives

By the end of this course you can:

  1. Describe the transition from the Mughal economy to colonial rule, 1707–1857.
  2. Explain what happened to agriculture, small-scale industry and large-scale industry under colonial rule, with numbers.
  3. Explain why the colonial government was small and what that meant for infrastructure and welfare.
  4. Explain why population growth began to rise in the 1910s and why labor did not move to better jobs on a large scale.
  5. Compare the economic records of British India and the princely states.
  6. Evaluate the development strategy of independent India and the turn of 1991.
  7. Weigh Roy's "composite" view against the drain view, with evidence.

Texts

  • Required: Tirthankar Roy, The Economic History of India, 1857–2010, 4th ed. (Oxford, 2020; in your library): Chapter 1 (introduction), Chapter 2 "Transition to Colonialism, 1707–1857", Chapter 3 (the growth record), Chapter 4 "Agriculture", Chapter 5 "Small-Scale Industry", Chapter 6 "Large-Scale Industry", Chapter 8 "Infrastructure", Chapter 9 "How the Government Worked", Chapter 10 "Population", Chapter 12 (the princely states), Chapter 13 (the Indian Union).
  • Primary source: Dadabhai Naoroji, Poverty and Un-British Rule in India (1901), excerpt.
