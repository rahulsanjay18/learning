## Course description
A graduate course on four regions whose histories don't fit the north-Indian story the survey courses tell: South India,
Bengal, the Punjab, and the Deccan. It draws mostly on the New Cambridge History of India volumes, several of which are already
in your library. Each region gets two weeks.

## Learning objectives
By the end of this course you can:
1. Tell the history of each region in its own terms, with its own turning points.
2. Explain how each region's ecology, trade and religion shaped its politics.
3. Explain how each region's history changes the national story you learned in Level I.

## Texts
- **Required (in your library):** Burton Stein, *Vijayanagara*; P. J. Marshall, *Bengal: The British Bridgehead*; J. S. Grewal, *The Sikhs of the Punjab*; M. N. Pearson, *The Portuguese in India* (all New Cambridge History of India).
- **Required (to buy):** Noboru Karashima (ed.), *A Concise History of South India* (Oxford, 2014). Richard Eaton, *A Social History of the Deccan, 1300–1761* (New Cambridge History, 2005).
