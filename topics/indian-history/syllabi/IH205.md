## Course description
The Indian freedom movement and the partition of the subcontinent, from the founding of the Congress in 1885 to August 1947.
It covers early nationalism, Gandhi's campaigns, the return to elections and constitutional politics, the Second World War and
Quit India, and the path to Partition. Partition gets two lessons: one on how it was decided and one on what it did to the
people who lived through it. This course connects most directly to family history, and the oral-history project (IH390) fits
well alongside it.

## Learning objectives
By the end of this course you can:
1. Explain the rise of nationalism and the growing split between Hindu and Muslim politics before 1920.
2. Explain satyagraha as a political strategy, and order Gandhi's major campaigns (1920, 1930, 1942).
3. Explain the Round Table conferences, the Communal Award and the 1937 elections, and why they mattered for Muslim politics.
4. Explain the idea of Pakistan, from Iqbal and Rahmat Ali to the Lahore Resolution.
5. Give at least three causes of Partition, weigh them against each other, and evaluate the "divide and rule" explanation.
6. Describe the violence and migration of 1947 and use literature and oral history as historical sources.

## Texts
- **Required:** Kulke and Rothermund, Chapter 7 "The Freedom Movement and the Partition of India". In your library.
- **Required (in your library):** Bhikhu Parekh, *Gandhi: A Very Short Introduction*. Manto's stories, including "Toba Tek Singh" (Aatish Taseer's translation).
- **Required (to buy):** Yasmin Khan, *The Great Partition* (2nd ed., 2017), by topic: 1946 to 1947.
- **Recommended (to buy):** Urvashi Butalia, *The Other Side of Silence* (1998), the opening chapter. Bose and Jalal, *Modern South Asia*, the nationalism chapters.
- **Primary sources:** Gandhi's statement at his trial (1922; in K&R); Jinnah's Lahore address (1940); the 1947 Partition Archive (online oral histories).
