Course description

An optional breadth course on Indian art, architecture and literature across eras: the Buddhist stupa and the Hindu temple, Sanskrit and Tamil classical literature, the epics, Mughal and Rajput painting, Indo-Islamic architecture, and the regional literatures of the colonial era. We study works as historical evidence (who paid for them, who they were for, what they claimed) as well as for their own sake.

Learning objectives

By the end of this course you can:

  1. Describe the main forms of Indian religious architecture and how they developed.
  2. Read a passage of the Mahabharata or Ramayana and say what it shows about values and power in its time.
  3. Explain the patronage and politics behind Mughal and Rajput painting.
  4. Describe the main features of Indo-Islamic architecture with examples.
  5. Explain the rise of literatures in regional languages during the colonial period.

Texts

  • Required (in your library): Debroy's translation of the Mahabharata (selected passages); Narayan, The Ramayana; Cleveland Beach, Mughal and Rajput Painting (New Cambridge History of India); Kulke and Rothermund, Ch. 2 "Kalidasa and classical Sanskrit literature", Ch. 3 "Literature and language", Ch. 6 "A national literature in regional languages"; Tagore, Gitanjali.
  • Recommended (to buy): Partha Mitter, Indian Art (Oxford History of Art, 2001).
  • Images: from museum collections online (credited in each lesson).
