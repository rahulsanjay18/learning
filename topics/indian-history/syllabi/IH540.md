## Course description
An optional graduate elective on Partition and how it has been remembered: in literature, oral history, film, and the politics of
India and Pakistan since 1947. Most of the reading is by historians who rely on oral history and literature, because official
archives say little about what Partition did to ordinary people, women especially.

## Learning objectives
By the end of this course you can:
1. Explain the turn in Partition studies from high politics to people's experience.
2. Use literature and oral testimony as historical evidence, with their limits.
3. Explain how Partition memory has been used in Indian and Pakistani politics.

## Texts
- **Required:** Urvashi Butalia, *The Other Side of Silence* (1998). Yasmin Khan, *The Great Partition* (2nd ed., 2017). Vazira Zamindar, *The Long Partition and the Making of Modern South Asia* (2007). Manto's stories, and Manto and Chughtai's *Essential Stories* (in your library). The 1947 Partition Archive (online).
