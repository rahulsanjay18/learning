Course description

A graduate course in reading primary sources from two thousand years of Indian history, in English translation. Each week takes one major source, reads a long extract closely, and places it in context: Megasthenes, Ashoka's edicts, the Arthashastra, Faxian, Alberuni, the Baburnama, the Ain-i-Akbari, and colonial records. For each one, the questions are what the translation changes, who the text was for, and how far historians can use it.

Learning objectives

By the end of this course you can:

  1. Read a long primary source closely and summarize its argument and its assumptions.
  2. Evaluate a translation: what it can't carry over, and how two translations differ.
  3. Use a source as evidence for something its author didn't intend to tell us.
  4. Corroborate a source against other evidence (archaeological, epigraphic, or another text).

Texts

  • Required: Megasthenes' Indica fragments (McCrindle's translation, public domain). Ashoka's edicts (in Romila Thapar, Aśoka and the Decline of the Mauryas, appendix, or Patrick Olivelle, Ashoka: Portrait of a Philosopher King). Kautilya's Arthashastra (Olivelle's translation, 2013). Faxian's record (Legge's translation, 1886). Alberuni's India (Sachau's translation, 1888). The Baburnama (Thackston's translation, 1996). Abu'l Fazl's Ain-i-Akbari (Blochmann and Jarrett's translation, public domain). Macaulay's Minute (1835) and a district gazetteer of your choice (public domain).
