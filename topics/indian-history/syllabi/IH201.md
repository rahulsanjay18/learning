## Course description
Ancient India, from the first farming villages to the Gupta empire and the early kingdoms of the south. We read Chapters 1 and 2
of Kulke and Rothermund in full, including the sections IH100 skipped. Alongside them we read Upinder Singh's textbook for depth,
and primary sources: Vedic hymns, the Upanishads, the *Arthashastra*, Ashoka's edicts, Faxian, and Tamil Sangam poetry. Two
arguments run through the course. One is about the Indo-Aryans: who they were, when they came, and why it is still politically
charged. The other is about whether the Guptas deserve the title of a "golden age".

## Learning objectives
By the end of this course you can:
1. Describe the Indus civilization's sites, economy and end, and evaluate the main theories of its decline.
2. Explain the evidence for Indo-Aryan migration (linguistic, textual, genetic) and evaluate the "out of India" alternative.
3. Explain how varna and jati developed, and what the Vedic texts can and can't tell us about society.
4. Explain the second urbanization in the Ganges valley and why Buddhism and Jainism arose in it.
5. Describe the Mauryan state and evaluate Ashoka's dhamma as policy and as belief.
6. Explain the Kushana and Gupta empires' structure, and judge the "golden age" label with evidence.
7. Describe early South India and the Indian Ocean trade with Rome.

## Texts
- **Required:** Kulke and Rothermund, *A History of India*, 6th ed., Chapter 1 "Early Civilisations of the Northwest" and Chapter 2 "The Great Ancient Empires". In your library.
- **Required (to buy):** Upinder Singh, *A History of Ancient and Early Medieval India: From the Stone Age to the 12th Century* (Pearson, 2008; 2nd ed. 2024), by topic: the Harappan civilization, the Vedic period, the age of the Buddha, the Mauryas, the post-Mauryan period, and the Guptas.
- **Recommended (in your library):** Easwaran, *The Upanishads*; Keown, *Buddhism: A Very Short Introduction*; Knott, *Hinduism: A Very Short Introduction*; Adamson and Ganeri, *Classical Indian Philosophy*, Chapters 3–10.
- **Primary sources in translation:** Rigveda hymns (in K&R); Olivelle's translation of the *Arthashastra* (2013; to buy, or excerpts I provide); Ashoka's edicts (K&R and Thapar's translation); Faxian's record (Legge's 1886 translation, public domain).
