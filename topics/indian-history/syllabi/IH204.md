## Course description
British rule in India, from the East India Company's takeover of Bengal to the constitutional reforms of the 1930s. We look at
the Company as a trading firm that became a government, then at law, land revenue and education, the uprising of 1857, the
colonial economy, how British rule differed between regions, and the reforms that set up the politics of independence. The
biggest economic debate here, whether and how much Britain "drained" India, gets a full week.

## Learning objectives
By the end of this course you can:
1. Explain how a trading company became the ruler of Bengal, and how Parliament came to regulate it.
2. Explain the Permanent Settlement and other revenue systems, and their effects on peasants and landholders.
3. Explain the debate over English education, and read Macaulay's Minute critically.
4. Give the causes of 1857 and explain what changed after it.
5. Describe the colonial economy (silver, railways, the fate of Indian industry) and evaluate the drain argument and the numbers attached to it.
6. Compare British impact in Bengal, Madras, Bombay and the Punjab.
7. Trace the constitutional reforms from Morley–Minto to the Government of India Act of 1935, including separate electorates.

## Texts
- **Required:** Kulke and Rothermund, Chapter 6 "The Period of Colonial Rule". In your library.
- **Required (in your library):** Tirthankar Roy, *The Economic History of India*, the chapters on the colonial period. Ashley Jackson, *The British Empire: A Very Short Introduction*.
- **Required (to buy):** Bose and Jalal, *Modern South Asia*, 4th ed., by topic: Company rule, the colonial state, 1857, and the colonial economy.
- **Recommended (in your library):** P. J. Marshall, *Bengal: The British Bridgehead* (New Cambridge History of India).
- **Primary sources:** Macaulay's Minute on Indian Education (1835; quoted in K&R); Queen Victoria's Proclamation of 1858; Dadabhai Naoroji, *Poverty and Un-British Rule in India* (1901), excerpt.
