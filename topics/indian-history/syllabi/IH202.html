Course description

India from about 600 to 1500: the regional kingdoms of the early medieval period, temple cities and bhakti devotion, India's cultural influence in Southeast Asia, the Turkish conquests, the Delhi Sultanate, and the states of the Deccan and the south, ending with Vijayanagara. This period has two of the most heated debates in Indian historiography: whether it should be called "feudal", and what the conquests meant for religion. The second is a live political question today.

Learning objectives

By the end of this course you can:

  1. Describe the regional kingdoms after Harsha and explain why no single power dominated the north.
  2. Explain how kings used samantas, Brahmin land grants and temple patronage to hold power, and evaluate the "Indian feudalism" thesis.
  3. Explain the rise of bhakti and of temple cities, and their political role.
  4. Describe India's influence in Southeast Asia and explain the competing theories of how it spread.
  5. Distinguish Mahmud of Ghazni's raids from the Ghurid conquest and the founding of the Delhi Sultanate, and explain how the Sultanate governed.
  6. Evaluate claims about temple destruction using Eaton's evidence and its critics.
  7. Explain the rise and fall of Vijayanagara.

Texts

  • Required: Kulke and Rothermund, Chapter 3 "The Regional Kingdoms of Early Medieval India" and Chapter 4 "Religious Communities and Military Feudalism in the Late Middle Ages". In your library.
  • Required (to buy): Upinder Singh, A History of Ancient and Early Medieval India, by topic: the early medieval period, c. 600–1200. Richard Eaton, India in the Persianate Age, 1000–1765 (2019), by topic: the Turkish conquests, the Delhi Sultanate, and the Deccan states.
  • Recommended (in your library): Burton Stein, Vijayanagara (New Cambridge History of India); Knott, Hinduism: A Very Short Introduction, Chapter 5 "The divine presence".
  • Primary sources: Alberuni's India (Sachau's 1888 translation, public domain), Chapter 1; a bhakti poem quoted in K&R.
  • Article: Richard Eaton, "Temple Desecration and Indo-Muslim States", Journal of Islamic Studies 11:3 (2000).
