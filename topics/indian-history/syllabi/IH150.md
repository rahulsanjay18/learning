## Course description
A short methods course: how historians know what they claim to know. We cover sourcing and corroboration, the difference
between facts and interpretations, what archaeology, inscriptions and texts can and can't tell us, and why the old division of
Indian history into "Hindu", "Muslim" and "British" periods is misleading. You've already been doing a version of this in IH100.
This course makes it explicit, so the era courses can go faster.

## Learning objectives
By the end of this course you can:
1. Apply the four historian's questions (sourcing, context, corroboration, close reading) to any document.
2. Explain the difference between a historical fact and an interpretation, with an example from Indian history.
3. Say what each kind of evidence (excavated material, inscriptions, coins, texts, oral testimony) is good for and where it misleads.
4. Explain where the "Hindu, Muslim, British" periodization came from and what replaced it.

## Texts
- **Required:** Digital Inquiry Group (formerly the Stanford History Education Group), *Reading Like a Historian* (free online): the "historical thinking chart" and two of its sample lessons.
- **Required:** E. H. Carr, *What Is History?* (1961; to buy, or any library copy), Chapter 1, "The Historian and His Facts".
- **Required:** Kulke and Rothermund, Introduction, "History and the Environment" (in your library), reread for its argument about periodization.
- **Recommended:** Bahn, *Archaeology: A Very Short Introduction* (in your library).
- **Optional:** Marc Bloch, *The Historian's Craft* (1953).
