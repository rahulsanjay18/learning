## Course description
India since 1947: making the constitution and absorbing the princely states, Nehru's planned economy and foreign policy,
redrawing states along language lines, Indira Gandhi and the Emergency, the end of single-party Congress rule, liberalization,
coalition politics, and the rise of the BJP. We also cover India's relations with China, Pakistan, the Soviet Union and the
United States. Kulke and Rothermund give the outline. Ramachandra Guha's *India After Gandhi* gives the detail.

## Learning objectives
By the end of this course you can:
1. Name the constitution's key choices (federal, secular, parliamentary, with fundamental rights) and the arguments behind them.
2. Explain how the princely states were integrated, and why Kashmir became the exception.
3. Explain the reorganization of states along linguistic lines and its effects.
4. Explain the Emergency (1975–77) as a test of those constitutional choices.
5. Describe the economic path from planning to the 1991 liberalization.
6. Explain the rise of Hindu nationalism as a political force, using its own writers and its critics.
7. Describe India's main foreign-policy turning points: 1962, 1971, 1974 and 1998.

## Texts
- **Required:** Kulke and Rothermund, Chapter 8 "The Republic", and "Perspectives". In your library.
- **Required (to buy):** Ramachandra Guha, *India After Gandhi* (revised editions 2017 and later): Part One "Picking Up the Pieces" (selected chapters: "Apples in the Basket", "Ideas of India"), Part Two "Nehru's India" ("The Biggest Gamble in History", "Redrawing the Map"), Part Four "The Rise of Populism" ("Autumn of the Matriarch").
- **Required (in your library):** Jyotirmaya Sharma, *Hindutva: Exploring the Idea of Hindu Nationalism*; Swapan Dasgupta, *Awakening Bharat Mata*, the introduction.
- **Primary sources:** Nehru, "Tryst with Destiny" (1947); the Preamble to the Constitution; B. R. Ambedkar's closing speech to the Constituent Assembly (25 November 1949).
