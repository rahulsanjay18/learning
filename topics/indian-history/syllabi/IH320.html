Course description

An optional breadth course on war and the state in India, from chariots and elephants to the colonial army. It pairs with the strategy course in the Games major. We read Indian strategic thought (the Arthashastra's circle of kings), study why some armies beat others (Panipat 1526, Plassey 1757, the drilled infantry of the eighteenth century), and look at how armies were paid for, since that question usually decided who won.

Learning objectives

By the end of this course you can:

  1. Explain the Arthashastra's theory of interstate relations (the mandala) and compare it with Western realism.
  2. Explain how the environment shaped Indian warfare (horses from the north, elephants, monsoon campaigning).
  3. Analyse a battle (Panipat 1526 or Plassey 1757) as a strategist: aims, means, and why it turned out as it did.
  4. Explain how the Mughal and Maratha states raised and paid armies, and why European-style infantry won in the eighteenth century.
  5. Explain the colonial army's recruitment policy ("martial races") and its political effects.

Texts

  • Required: Kulke and Rothermund (in your library): Introduction, "Chariots, Elephants and the Methods of Warfare"; Ch. 2 "Rajamandala, an image of early Indian geo-politics"; Ch. 5 "Sticking to his guns" and "European military intervention: infantry versus cavalry"; Ch. 6 "The Panjab and the martial races" and "The role of the army and the 'great game'".
  • Required (to buy): Jos Gommans, Mughal Warfare: Indian Frontiers and Highroads to Empire, 1500–1700 (Routledge, 2002), by topic.
  • Recommended (in your library): Echevarria, Military Strategy: A Very Short Introduction, for the vocabulary you already use in the Games major.
  • Primary sources: the Arthashastra (Olivelle's translation), Book 6 on the circle of kings; the Baburnama on Panipat.
