Course description

An optional breadth course on the religious and philosophical traditions of India across 3,000 years: Vedic religion and the Upanishads, Buddhism and Jainism, the classical schools of philosophy, devotional Hinduism, Islam and Sufism in India, Sikhism, and the reform movements of the colonial era. The emphasis is historical: how each tradition changed, and how it connected to political power.

Learning objectives

By the end of this course you can:

  1. Explain the main ideas of the Upanishads (self, karma, liberation) and how Buddhism and Jainism answered them.
  2. Name the six classical schools of Hindu philosophy and the central claim of each.
  3. Explain the rise of bhakti and how it changed religious practice and authority.
  4. Describe how Islam and Sufism took root in India, and the origins of Sikhism.
  5. Explain the colonial-era reform movements and the modern idea of "Hinduism" as a single religion.

Texts

  • Required (in your library): Knott, Hinduism: A Very Short Introduction, Chapters 1 "The scholar and the devotee", 2 "Revelation and the transmission of knowledge", 3 "Understanding the self", 6 "Hinduism, colonialism, and modernity", 9 "Hindu dharma, Hinduism, and hinduisms", and the appendix on the six schools. Keown, Buddhism: A Very Short Introduction. Nesbitt, Sikhism: A Very Short Introduction. Ruthven, Islam: A Very Short Introduction. Adamson and Ganeri, Classical Indian Philosophy, Chapters 4–9 and 19–25.
  • Recommended (in your library): Hamilton, Indian Philosophy: A Very Short Introduction; Easwaran, The Upanishads; Dasgupta, A History of Indian Philosophy, for reference.
