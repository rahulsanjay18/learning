## Course description
A survey of all of Indian history, from the environment and the Indus cities to the Republic, in sixteen short lessons. Each
lesson reads one or two key sections of Kulke and Rothermund and leaves the rest for the era courses. The goal is a timeline and a
mental map you can hang everything else on. Every lesson includes one primary source and, most weeks, a present-day claim about
the period to judge against the evidence.

## Learning objectives
By the end of this course you can:
1. Place the major periods, dynasties and turning points on a timeline, including the dates listed in the Notes.
2. Locate the main sites, capitals and regions on a map.
3. Give at least two causes for each major change: the end of the Indus cities, the rise of the Mauryas, the Delhi Sultanate, Mughal decline, British rule, Partition.
4. Read a primary source critically: who wrote it, for whom, why, and how far to trust it.
5. Tell invasion from migration, and raid from state-building, when you meet these claims in public debate.
6. Write a 300-word argued answer to a "why" question, with a counter-argument.

## Texts
- **Required:** Kulke and Rothermund, *A History of India*, 6th ed. (Routledge, 2016). In your library. The ebook has no page numbers, so readings are given by section heading.
- **Primary sources** (excerpts in the lessons): the Rigveda's Purusha hymn; Ashoka's Rock Edict XIII; Faxian's account of Gupta India; Alberuni's *India*; the *Baburnama*; Macaulay's Minute on Education (1835); Gandhi's statement at his trial (1922); Jinnah's Lahore address (1940); Nehru's "Tryst with Destiny" (1947).

## Notes
Dates to know by the end (they come back in daily review until you have them): about 2600–1700 BC, the Indus cities; about
1380 BC, the Mitanni treaty naming Vedic gods; about 321 BC, the Mauryas; about 268 BC, Ashoka; about AD 320, the Guptas; 1206,
the Delhi Sultanate; 1526, Panipat; 1757, Plassey; 1857; 1885, the Indian National Congress; 1920, non-cooperation; 1947. The
lesson-by-lesson objectives are in the major's syllabus file.
