Named distributions

Geometric: trials until the first success

\(P(N=k) = (1-p)^{k-1}p\),   \(E[N] = \frac{1}{p}\),   \(\operatorname{Var}(N) = \frac{1-p}{p^2}\)

Why \(1/p\): a success rate of \(p\) per trial means one success per \(1/p\) trials on average.

Poisson(\(\lambda\)): counts of rare events in a fixed window

\(P(X=k) = \frac{\lambda^k e^{-\lambda}}{k!}\),   \(E[X] = \operatorname{Var}(X) = \lambda\),   so \(P(X=0) = e^{-\lambda}\)

Mean equals variance: a quick check for whether count data are plausibly Poisson.

Exponential(\(\lambda\)): waiting time between Poisson events

\(P(T > t) = e^{-\lambda t}\),   \(E[T] = \frac{1}{\lambda}\). Memoryless: \(P(T > s+t \mid T > s) = \frac{e^{-\lambda(s+t)}}{e^{-\lambda s}} = e^{-\lambda t} = P(T > t)\).

The continuous twin of the geometric; the only memoryless continuous distribution.

Sampling

Standard error of a mean

\(\operatorname{Var}(\bar X) = \frac{\sigma^2}{n}\), so \(\operatorname{SE}(\bar X) = \frac{\sigma}{\sqrt n}\)

Variances of \(n\) independent terms add (\(n\sigma^2\)), then dividing by \(n\) scales variance by \(1/n^2\). Four times the data halves the error.

Regression

Simple least squares

\(\hat\beta_1 = \frac{\sum (x_i-\bar x)(y_i-\bar y)}{\sum (x_i-\bar x)^2} = r\,\frac{s_y}{s_x}\),   \(\hat\beta_0 = \bar y - \hat\beta_1 \bar x\),   \(R^2 = r^2\)

The slope is the correlation rescaled from standard-deviation units into the units of \(y\) per unit of \(x\).

Bayesian

Beta–binomial updating

Prior \(\text{Beta}(a,b)\), then \(k\) successes in \(n\) trials gives posterior \(\text{Beta}(a+k,\; b+n-k)\), mean \(\frac{a+k}{a+b+n}\).

\(a\) and \(b\) act like prior successes and failures; data just add to the counts.

Testing

p-value

\(p = P(\text{result at least this extreme} \mid H_0)\). It is not \(P(H_0 \mid \text{data})\), and it isn't an effect size. See lesson 2.

Two-sided test at \(\alpha = 0.05\): reject when \(|z| > 1.96\).
