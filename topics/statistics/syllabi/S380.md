## Course description
An optional course that connects this major to your machine learning work. It uses *An Introduction to Statistical Learning*:
resampling, model selection and regularization, nonlinear models, trees and ensembles, support vector machines, unsupervised
learning, and multiple testing. You already use many of these methods, so the course focuses on the statistical side: bias and
variance, what the error estimates mean, and when the methods fail.

## Learning objectives
By the end of this course you can:
1. Explain the bias–variance tradeoff for a given method and choose flexibility with cross-validation.
2. Derive ridge regression and explain why the lasso produces exact zeros.
3. Fit splines and generalized additive models and interpret them.
4. Explain why bagging, random forests and boosting reduce error, and in which part of the error.
5. Explain the support vector classifier as an optimization problem.
6. Use principal components and clustering, and explain what they can't tell you.
7. Control the false discovery rate when testing many hypotheses.

## Texts
- **Required:** James, Witten, Hastie and Tibshirani, *An Introduction to Statistical Learning*, 2nd ed. (2021; free from the authors; the Python edition, 2023, has the same chapters): Chapters 2, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 12, 13.
- **Optional:** Hastie, Tibshirani and Friedman, *The Elements of Statistical Learning*, for the proofs ISL leaves out.
