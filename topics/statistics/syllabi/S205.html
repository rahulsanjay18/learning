Course description

Chapter 5 of Casella and Berger, where probability turns into statistics. It covers random samples and statistics, sums of random variables, sampling from the normal distribution (including why the sample mean and variance are independent, and where Student's t and the F distribution come from), order statistics, the different kinds of convergence, the laws of large numbers, the central limit theorem, Slutsky's theorem, the delta method, and how a computer generates random samples. The tests and intervals in Chapters 8–10 depend on these results.

The pretest takes two meetings.

Learning objectives

By the end of this course you can:

  1. Prove that the sample mean and sample variance are unbiased, and use mgfs to find the distribution of a sum.
  2. Prove that the sample mean and variance are independent for normal data, and derive the t and F distributions.
  3. Find the distribution of an order statistic.
  4. Tell convergence in probability, almost sure convergence and convergence in distribution apart, with examples.
  5. Prove the weak law of large numbers, state the central limit theorem precisely, and use Slutsky's theorem.
  6. Use the delta method to get an approximate distribution.
  7. Generate samples by inverting the distribution function and by accept/reject, and explain why accept/reject works.

Texts

  • Required: Casella and Berger, 2nd ed., Chapter 5, "Properties of a Random Sample", pp. 207–255. In your library.
  • Recommended: Bruce, Bruce and Gedeck, Practical Statistics for Data Scientists, 2nd ed. (in your library), Chapter 2, "Data and Sampling Distributions", for the applied side: standard errors and the bootstrap.
