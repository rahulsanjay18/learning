## Course description
Asymptotic statistics from van der Vaart's book of that name, the standard graduate text. It covers stochastic convergence, the
delta method, M- and Z-estimators, contiguity and local asymptotic normality, efficiency, U-statistics, likelihood ratio and
chi-square tests, empirical processes, and the bootstrap. It explains why large-sample methods work, and it underpins
semiparametric methods and much of machine learning theory.

## Learning objectives
By the end of this course you can:
1. Use the continuous mapping theorem, Slutsky's lemma, Prohorov's theorem and the delta method in proofs.
2. Prove consistency and asymptotic normality of M- and Z-estimators.
3. Explain local asymptotic normality and use it to state what "efficient" means for an estimator.
4. Derive the limit of a U-statistic by projection.
5. Derive the asymptotic distribution of likelihood ratio and Pearson chi-square tests.
6. State the Glivenko–Cantelli and Donsker theorems for empirical processes and use them on a simple estimator.
7. Explain when the bootstrap is consistent.

## Texts
- **Required:** van der Vaart, *Asymptotic Statistics* (Cambridge, 1998; to buy): Chapter 2 "Stochastic Convergence", Chapter 3 "Delta Method", Chapter 5 "M- and Z-Estimators", Chapter 6 "Contiguity", Chapter 7 "Local Asymptotic Normality", Chapter 8 "Efficiency of Estimators", Chapter 11 "Projections", Chapter 12 "U-Statistics", Chapter 16 "Likelihood Ratio Tests", Chapter 17 "Chi-Square Tests", Chapter 19 "Empirical Processes", Chapter 23 "Bootstrap".
