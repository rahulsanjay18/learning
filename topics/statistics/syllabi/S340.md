## Course description
An introduction to Bayesian statistics using the first part of Gelman et al., *Bayesian Data Analysis*. We cover single-parameter
and multiparameter models, conjugate priors, how Bayesian and classical answers line up in large samples, and hierarchical
models, the main reason applied statisticians use Bayesian methods. You'll fit models in Python with PyMC as well as by hand.

## Learning objectives
By the end of this course you can:
1. Derive the posterior for the beta–binomial, normal–normal and Poisson–gamma models, and explain each prior's role.
2. Summarize a posterior with intervals and predictive distributions, and check a model with posterior predictive checks.
3. Handle a multiparameter model, including the normal model with unknown mean and variance, and marginalize out nuisance parameters.
4. Explain why posteriors approach normal distributions centered at the maximum likelihood estimate in large samples, and when that fails.
5. Build a hierarchical model, explain partial pooling, and fit it with MCMC.

## Texts
- **Required:** Gelman, Carlin, Stern, Dunson, Vehtari and Rubin, *Bayesian Data Analysis*, 3rd ed. (CRC, 2013; free PDF from the authors): Chapter 1 "Probability and inference", Chapter 2 "Single-parameter models", Chapter 3 "Introduction to multiparameter models", Chapter 4 "Asymptotics and connections to non-Bayesian approaches", Chapter 5 "Hierarchical models".
- **Recommended:** Casella and Berger §7.2.3, §8.2.2 and §9.2.4, the Bayesian sections you'll already have read.
- **Optional:** Jaynes, *Probability Theory: The Logic of Science* (in your library), Chapters 1–2, for the case for Bayesian reasoning.
