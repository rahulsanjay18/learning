Course description

Statistical learning theory and high-dimensional statistics: what happens when the number of parameters is as large as the sample, or larger. It covers concentration inequalities, uniform laws and complexity measures, random matrices, the lasso and sparse regression, high-dimensional covariance and PCA, kernel methods, and minimax lower bounds. Wainwright's book is the main text, Vershynin's for the probability, and The Elements of Statistical Learning for the methods side. This course is the most directly useful for reading ML theory papers.

Learning objectives

By the end of this course you can:

  1. Prove sub-Gaussian and Bernstein tail bounds and use them to bound the error of an estimator.
  2. Bound uniform deviations with Rademacher complexity and metric entropy.
  3. Prove an error bound for the lasso under a restricted eigenvalue condition.
  4. Bound the operator-norm error of a sample covariance matrix and explain when PCA fails in high dimensions.
  5. Explain reproducing kernel Hilbert spaces and the representer theorem.
  6. Prove a minimax lower bound with Fano's or Le Cam's method.

Texts

  • Required: Wainwright, High-Dimensional Statistics: A Non-Asymptotic Viewpoint (Cambridge, 2019; to buy): Chapters 2 "Basic tail and concentration bounds", 4 "Uniform laws of large numbers", 5 "Metric entropy and its uses", 6 "Random matrices and covariance estimation", 7 "Sparse linear models in high dimensions", 8 "Principal component analysis in high dimensions", 12 "Reproducing kernel Hilbert spaces", 15 "Minimax lower bounds".
  • Recommended: Vershynin, High-Dimensional Probability (Cambridge, 2018; in your library), Chapters 2–4, for the concentration results with more intuition.
  • Recommended: Hastie, Tibshirani and Friedman, The Elements of Statistical Learning, 2nd ed. (free from the authors), Chapter 18 "High-Dimensional Problems: p ≫ N", for the methods.
