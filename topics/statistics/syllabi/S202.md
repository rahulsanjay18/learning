## Course description
Chapter 2 of Casella and Berger. It covers how a distribution changes when you transform a random variable, expectation, moments
and moment generating functions, and when it's legal to differentiate under an integral sign. That last topic looks technical,
but you'll need it for moment generating functions here and for the Cramér–Rao bound in S302.

The course opens with a pretest on §2.1–2.4. You skip any section you get fully right. The chapter summary and check always happen.

## Learning objectives
By the end of this course you can:
1. Find the distribution of Y = g(X) when g is monotone or piecewise monotone, and prove the change-of-variables formula.
2. Prove that F(X) is uniform when X is continuous, and use that fact to generate random variables.
3. Compute expectations and use E[g(X)] without first finding the distribution of g(X).
4. Get moments from a moment generating function, and identify a distribution from its moment generating function, stating when that works.
5. State when you may differentiate under an integral or a sum, and check the condition in an example.

## Texts
- **Required:** Casella and Berger, 2nd ed., Chapter 2, "Transformations and Expectations", pp. 47–76. In your library.
- **Recommended:** Blitzstein and Hwang, *Introduction to Probability*, 2nd ed. (free from the authors), Chapter 6 "Moments" and Chapter 8 "Transformations", for more worked examples.
- **Optional:** Abbott, *Understanding Analysis* (in your library), if you want to review uniform convergence before §2.4.
