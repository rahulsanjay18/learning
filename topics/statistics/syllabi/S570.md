## Course description
An optional graduate elective on computational statistics: the theory behind Monte Carlo methods, MCMC and the EM algorithm.
S330 taught you to use these. This course proves why they work and when they don't: convergence of Markov chains used for
sampling, variance reduction, and convergence diagnostics.

## Learning objectives
By the end of this course you can:
1. Generate random variables by transformation, accept/reject and importance sampling, and bound their efficiency.
2. Reduce Monte Carlo variance with control variates, antithetic variables and Rao–Blackwellization.
3. Prove that Metropolis–Hastings has the target as its stationary distribution, and explain the conditions for convergence.
4. Build Gibbs samplers, including with data augmentation, and analyse one simple case.
5. Diagnose convergence and explain what the diagnostics can and can't show.
6. Prove that EM increases the likelihood and describe its rate of convergence.

## Texts
- **Required:** Robert and Casella, *Monte Carlo Statistical Methods*, 2nd ed. (Springer, 2004; to buy): Chapters 2 "Random Variable Generation", 3 "Monte Carlo Integration", 4 "Controlling Monte Carlo Variance", 6 "Markov Chains", 7 "The Metropolis–Hastings Algorithm", 9–10 on the Gibbs sampler, 12 "Diagnosing Convergence".
- **Recommended:** Efron and Hastie, *Computer Age Statistical Inference* (free), Chapters 9 and 13.
