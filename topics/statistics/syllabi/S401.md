## Course description
Chapter 10 of Casella and Berger: what happens as the sample size grows. It covers consistency and efficiency of estimators,
bootstrap standard errors, robustness (the mean against the median, and M-estimators), the large-sample distribution of the
likelihood ratio test, Wald and score tests, and approximate intervals built from them. Most of the standard errors and p-values
that statistical software prints come from this chapter.

## Learning objectives
By the end of this course you can:
1. Prove that the maximum likelihood estimator is consistent and asymptotically normal under stated regularity conditions, and compute its asymptotic variance from the Fisher information.
2. Compute a bootstrap standard error and explain when it is better than a delta-method one.
3. Compare the mean and the median by asymptotic relative efficiency and by breakdown point, and explain what an M-estimator buys you.
4. Prove that −2 log λ is asymptotically chi-squared for a simple null hypothesis, and give its degrees of freedom in general.
5. Build Wald, score and likelihood-ratio tests and intervals for the same problem, and say when they disagree.

## Texts
- **Required:** Casella and Berger, 2nd ed., Chapter 10, "Asymptotic Evaluations", pp. 467–504. In your library.
- **Recommended:** Efron and Hastie, *Computer Age Statistical Inference* (free from the authors), Chapter 10, "The Jackknife and the Bootstrap".
