## Overview
This major teaches mathematical statistics the way a good undergraduate program does, and then, if you choose, the first years of a
graduate program. The main text is Casella and Berger's *Statistical Inference* (2nd edition), read from the first chapter to the
last. You will write the proofs yourself. After that come four applied courses: regression, experiments and A/B testing,
statistical computing, and Bayesian statistics.

Statistics meets four times a week. Each lesson takes two meetings: one to read about seven pages, one to work through the lesson
and write a proof. Every chapter opens with a pretest. You have a math degree, so expect to skip a lot of the early probability.

## What you'll be able to do
**After Level I:**
- Design an A/B test, run it, and defend the result to a skeptical reader.
- Read p-values and confidence intervals correctly and explain the usual mistakes.
- Prove the main results of mathematical statistics: sufficiency, properties of maximum likelihood, the Neyman–Pearson lemma,
  interval construction, and large-sample theory.
- Fit a regression or a generalized linear model, interpret it and say what's wrong with it.
- Build and check a simple Bayesian model.

**After Level II (optional):**
- Read a statistics or machine-learning theory paper and check its proofs.
- Prove limit theorems with measure theory.
- Use causal-inference methods: potential outcomes, causal graphs, weighting and matching.
- Work at a first-year PhD level on estimation, testing, high-dimensional problems and adaptive experiments.

## How the major is organized
**Required courses (Level I).** S201–S205 cover Chapters 1–5 (probability). S301–S304 cover Chapters 6–9 (inference). S401–S403
cover Chapters 10–12 (large-sample theory and regression). Then S310 applied regression, S320 experiments and A/B testing, S330
statistical computing, and S340 Bayesian statistics.

**Optional courses.** S190 history and philosophy of statistics, S195 measurement and ethics, three electives (S360 time series,
S370 survey sampling, S380 statistical learning), S390 three real analyses from start to finish, S399 a capstone report, and S398 a
closing conversation about what you read.

**Level II** is optional and only starts if you sign up for it: S500 analysis review, S501–S502 probability theory, S511–S513
estimation, testing and asymptotics, S520 linear model theory, S530 Bayesian data analysis, S540 causal inference, S550
high-dimensional statistics, electives S560–S580, S590 foundations seminar, S595–S596 a qualifying-exam-style check and a research
project, and S598/S599 the closing conversation and thesis.

**Pretests.** Each chapter's pretest asks one concept question, one calculation and one proof for every section. A section you
get fully right is skipped. Get the whole pretest right and the chapter closes after a one-page summary and the chapter check.

## Order
Required courses run one at a time, in this order: S201 → S202 → S203 → S204 → S205 → S301 → S302 → S303 → S304 → S401 → S402 →
S403 → S310 → S320 → S330 → S340. S330 only needs S205, so it can move earlier if you want a break from proofs. Optional courses
fit in wherever you want them. S190 is light enough for a day you're tired.

The dates below assume no lessons get skipped. They're recalculated every time these pages are rebuilt, and pretests usually
pull them earlier, especially in Chapters 1–4.

## What it takes to finish
- **Level I:** S201–S205, S301–S304, S401–S403, S310, S320, S330 and S340, each finished through lessons or by passing its pretest.
- **Level II:** S500, S501, S502, S511, S512, S513, S520, S530, S540 and S550.

Athanasopoulos, *Forecasting* (2nd edition); Abbott, *Understanding Analysis*; Fremlin, *Measure Theory*; Vershynin,
*High-Dimensional Probability*; Jaynes, *Probability Theory*.

**Free online, please download before spring 2028:** James, Witten, Hastie and Tibshirani, *An Introduction to Statistical
Learning* (2nd edition; your copy is unreadable); Efron and Hastie, *Computer Age Statistical Inference*; Gelman et al.,
*Bayesian Data Analysis* (3rd edition); Hyndman and Athanasopoulos, *Forecasting* (3rd edition). For Level II: Durrett,
*Probability: Theory and Examples*; Hernán and Robins, *Causal Inference: What If*; Hastie, Tibshirani and Friedman, *The
Elements of Statistical Learning*; Lattimore and Szepesvári, *Bandit Algorithms*.

Xu, *Trustworthy Online Controlled Experiments* (S320); Salsburg, *The Lady Tasting Tea* (S190, any time). For Level II: Lehmann
and Casella; Lehmann and Romano; van der Vaart; Keener; Imbens and Rubin; Wainwright; Lohr; Wasserman; Mayo; Robert and Casella.
