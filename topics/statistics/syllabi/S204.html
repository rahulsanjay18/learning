Course description

Chapter 4 of Casella and Berger, the longest of the probability chapters. It covers joint, marginal and conditional distributions; independence; transformations of two variables; hierarchical models and mixtures; covariance and correlation; the multivariate normal and the multinomial; and the classic inequalities (Hölder, Cauchy–Schwarz, Minkowski and Jensen). The two results you'll use most often afterward are the law of iterated expectation and the conditional variance formula.

The pretest takes two meetings because the chapter is long.

Learning objectives

By the end of this course you can:

  1. Find marginal and conditional distributions from a joint distribution, and prove independence by factoring.
  2. Transform a pair of random variables using the Jacobian, including the case where the map isn't one-to-one.
  3. Prove E X = E[E(X | Y)] and Var X = E[Var(X | Y)] + Var(E[X | Y]), and use them in a hierarchical model.
  4. Prove that a correlation lies between −1 and 1, and that it equals ±1 only when the relationship is exactly linear.
  5. Work with the bivariate normal and the multinomial, including their marginal and conditional distributions.
  6. Prove the Cauchy–Schwarz and Jensen inequalities and use them to bound moments.

Texts

  • Required: Casella and Berger, 2nd ed., Chapter 4, "Multiple Random Variables", pp. 139–192. In your library.
  • Recommended: Blitzstein and Hwang, Introduction to Probability, 2nd ed. (free from the authors), Chapter 7 "Joint Distributions" and Chapter 9 "Conditional Expectation".
