## Course description
Statistics that needs a computer to work. We cover simulation, the bootstrap, cross-validation, the EM algorithm in practice, and
Markov chain Monte Carlo, all from Efron and Hastie's *Computer Age Statistical Inference*. It explains how these methods
replaced or extended the classical formulas. Every topic is written as working Python code, and every method is checked on a
case where you already know the right answer.

## Learning objectives
By the end of this course you can:
1. Write a simulation that estimates a probability or an error rate, with a standard error for the estimate.
2. Compute jackknife and bootstrap standard errors, and percentile and BCa bootstrap intervals, and say when each fails.
3. Estimate prediction error with cross-validation and with Cp, and explain the difference.
4. Implement EM for a missing-data or mixture problem.
5. Write a Metropolis–Hastings and a Gibbs sampler, and check convergence with trace plots and effective sample size.
6. Explain the false discovery rate and apply Benjamini–Hochberg to a large set of tests.

## Texts
- **Required:** Efron and Hastie, *Computer Age Statistical Inference* (Cambridge, 2016; free from the authors): Chapter 10 "The Jackknife and the Bootstrap", Chapter 11 "Bootstrap Confidence Intervals", Chapter 12 "Cross-Validation and Cp Estimates of Prediction Error", Chapter 9 "Survival Analysis and the EM Algorithm" (EM part), Chapter 13 "Objective Bayes Inference and MCMC", Chapter 15 "Large-Scale Hypothesis Testing and FDRs".
- **Recommended:** Casella and Berger §5.6 (generating random samples), already read in S205.
- **Software:** Python with NumPy and SciPy.
