## Course description
An optional short course on what it means to measure something, and the ways measurements go wrong before any statistics are
done. We'll use David Hand's *Measurement*. Hand is a statistician, and the book covers measurement in physics, medicine,
psychology and economics. We'll also spend one week on ethics, which comes up in your own work: consent, privacy, and what an
experiment does to the people in it.

## Learning objectives
By the end of this course you can:
1. Tell representational measurement from pragmatic measurement, with an example of each.
2. Spot measurement problems in a metric (validity, reliability, proxy drift) before analysing the data.
3. Name the main ethical questions in running experiments on users, and say how a review process handles them.

## Texts
- **Required:** Hand, *Measurement: A Very Short Introduction* (Oxford, 2016; in your library): Chapter 1 "A brief history", Chapter 2 "What is measurement?", Chapter 5 "Measurement in the behavioural sciences", Chapter 6 "Measurement in the social sciences, economics, business, and public policy", Chapter 7 "Measurement and understanding".
- **Recommended:** Kohavi, Tang and Xu, *Trustworthy Online Controlled Experiments* (to buy for S320), Chapter 9 "Ethics in Controlled Experiments".
