## Course description
The theory behind the models from S310, at graduate level, from Agresti's *Foundations of Linear and Generalized Linear Models*.
It covers least squares as projection, distribution theory for normal linear models, the general linear hypothesis, GLM fitting
and inference in full, quasi-likelihood, models for correlated responses (GEE and mixed models), and Bayesian versions of these
models.

## Learning objectives
By the end of this course you can:
1. Derive least squares as an orthogonal projection, and prove the distribution of quadratic forms in normal vectors (Cochran's theorem).
2. Test a general linear hypothesis and derive the F-test from the projection geometry.
3. Derive the likelihood equations and Fisher scoring for any GLM, and the asymptotic distribution of its estimates.
4. Use deviance and quasi-likelihood for overdispersed data.
5. Fit marginal (GEE) and conditional (mixed-effects) models for clustered data, and explain how their coefficients differ.
6. Write a Bayesian linear model and compare its estimates with the classical ones.

## Texts
- **Required:** Agresti, *Foundations of Linear and Generalized Linear Models* (Wiley, 2015; to buy): Chapters 2–4, 8, 9, 10.
