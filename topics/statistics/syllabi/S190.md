## Course description
An optional reading course on where statistics came from and the arguments that shaped it. Fisher, Pearson and Neyman disagreed
sharply about what a test even is, and much of today's confusion about p-values goes back to those fights. We also look at the
Bayesian–frequentist split. It's light reading, suited to days when you have energy for a story but not for a proof.

## Learning objectives
By the end of this course you can:
1. Describe Fisher's significance testing and Neyman–Pearson hypothesis testing, and say how the modern hybrid mixes them up.
2. Explain the main Bayesian and frequentist positions on what probability means, and give the strongest argument for each.
3. Place the key people and ideas (Bayes, Laplace, Galton, Pearson, Gosset, Fisher, Neyman, Tukey) on a timeline.

## Texts
- **Required:** Hand, *Statistics: A Very Short Introduction* (Oxford, 2008; in your library): Chapter 1 "Surrounded by statistics", Chapter 4 "Probability", Chapter 5 "Estimation and inference".
- **Required:** Salsburg, *The Lady Tasting Tea* (2001; to buy): the chapters on Fisher, Gosset, and Neyman and Pearson.
- **Optional:** Jaynes, *Probability Theory: The Logic of Science* (in your library), Preface and Chapter 1. Breiman, "Statistical Modeling: The Two Cultures", *Statistical Science*, 2001 (free online).
