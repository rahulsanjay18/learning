Course description

An optional conversation at the end of Level I. You put together a list of the 20 to 25 works you read, from textbooks to a few classic papers. You write a short rationale for how they fit together, around 1,500 to 2,500 words. Then we talk about it for an hour. It isn't an exam. It's a way to connect what you've learned, and people who do it tend to remember the material better.

Learning objectives

By the end of this course you can:

  1. Explain how the works on your list relate to each other across the history and branches of statistics.
  2. Defend a position on a central question (for example, what a statistical test is for) and answer objections to it.
  3. Describe how your plan changed during Level I and why.

Texts

  • Required: your own list of works. Candidates beyond the textbooks: Bayes (1763); Fisher, Statistical Methods for Research Workers (1925); Neyman and Pearson (1933); Tukey, "The Future of Data Analysis" (1962); Breiman, "Statistical Modeling: The Two Cultures" (2001).
