## Course description
Chapter 11 of Casella and Berger: one-way analysis of variance and simple linear regression, with every result derived. For ANOVA
we cover the model, contrasts, the F test, simultaneous inference (Scheffé) and partitioning sums of squares. For regression we
treat least squares first as a plain math problem and then as statistics. That means best linear unbiased estimators, inference
with normal errors, prediction at a new x, and confidence bands. S310 then takes the same models into real data.

## Learning objectives
By the end of this course you can:
1. Write the one-way ANOVA model, derive the F test, and partition the total sum of squares.
2. Test and estimate contrasts, and use Scheffé's method for simultaneous intervals.
3. Derive the least squares estimates and prove the Gauss–Markov property (they are best among linear unbiased estimators).
4. Derive the sampling distributions of the slope, intercept and residual variance under normal errors.
5. Tell a confidence interval for the mean response from a prediction interval for a new observation, and compute both.
6. Build a simultaneous confidence band for the regression line.

## Texts
- **Required:** Casella and Berger, 2nd ed., Chapter 11, "Analysis of Variance and Regression", pp. 521–563. In your library.
- **Recommended:** James et al., *An Introduction to Statistical Learning*, 2nd ed. (free from the authors), Chapter 3, "Linear Regression", for the applied view.
