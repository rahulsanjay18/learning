## Course description
Chapter 9 of Casella and Berger: interval estimation. It covers four ways to build a confidence set (inverting a test, pivotal
quantities, pivoting the distribution function, and Bayesian intervals) and four ways to judge one (size and coverage, optimality
tied to tests, Bayesian optimality, and loss functions). After this course you should be able to explain a confidence interval to
someone who doesn't trust them, and say exactly where the 95% comes from.

## Learning objectives
By the end of this course you can:
1. Build a confidence set by inverting a family of tests, and prove that its coverage equals one minus the tests' size.
2. Find a pivotal quantity and use it to build an interval.
3. Build an interval by pivoting a distribution function, including for a discrete distribution.
4. Compute a Bayesian credible interval and explain how its meaning differs from a confidence interval's.
5. Find the shortest interval in a family and say when "shortest" is the right goal.

## Texts
