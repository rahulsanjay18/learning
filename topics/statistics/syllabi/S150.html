Course description

A short course on reading statistical results correctly: what a p-value is and isn't, effect sizes and confidence intervals, and power. It closed early on October 6, 2026, so the major could start Casella and Berger from Chapter 1. The three lessons it didn't reach (likelihood ratios, multiple testing, and a full A/B test) moved to S303, where Chapter 8 covers the same material in more depth.

Learning objectives

  1. Say what a p-value is the probability of, and name three common misreadings.
  2. Report an effect with its confidence interval and explain what the 95% refers to.
  3. Compute the power of a one-sided z-test and say how sample size, effect size and significance level change it.
  4. Explain why Casella and Berger's β means power while most applied books use β for the miss rate.

Texts

  • Required: Casella and Berger, Statistical Inference, 2nd ed. (in your library): §9.1, and §8.3.1, pp. 382–385.
  • Recommended: Bruce, Bruce and Gedeck, Practical Statistics for Data Scientists, 2nd ed. (in your library), Chapter 3, "Statistical Experiments and Significance Testing".
