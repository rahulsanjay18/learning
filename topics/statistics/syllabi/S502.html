Course description

The second half of graduate probability: martingales, Markov chains, ergodic theorems and Brownian motion, from Durrett Chapters 4–8. Martingales are the tool that matters most here for statistics and machine learning. They are behind sequential testing, the analysis of stochastic gradient methods, and the concentration inequalities used in learning theory.

Learning objectives

By the end of this course you can:

  1. Define conditional expectation with respect to a sigma-algebra and prove its main properties.
  2. Prove the martingale convergence theorem and the optional stopping theorem, and apply them.
  3. Prove Doob's inequality and use martingales to analyse a sequential test.
  4. Classify the states of a Markov chain, find stationary distributions, and prove convergence for an aperiodic, irreducible chain.
  5. Construct Brownian motion in outline and prove its basic path properties.

Texts

  • Required: Durrett, Probability: Theory and Examples, 5th ed. (free PDF from the author): Chapter 4 "Martingales", Chapter 5 "Markov Chains", Chapter 6 "Ergodic Theorems", Chapter 7 "Brownian Motion", Chapter 8 "Applications to Random Walk" (selected sections).
