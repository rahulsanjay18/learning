## Course description
Causal inference from observational and experimental data, from Hernán and Robins's *Causal Inference: What If*, with Imbens and
Rubin for the potential-outcomes theory of experiments. It covers potential outcomes, randomized experiments, confounding and
selection bias with causal graphs, inverse probability weighting, standardization and the g-formula, propensity scores,
instrumental variables, and time-varying treatments. Your placement showed that you could name a confounder but reached for an
observational fix, so this is the course that closes that gap fully.

## Learning objectives
By the end of this course you can:
1. Define causal effects with potential outcomes and state the assumptions (exchangeability, positivity, consistency) needed to identify them.
2. Draw a causal graph and use it to decide which variables to adjust for, including how to recognize colliders and selection bias.
3. Estimate a causal effect by inverse probability weighting, by standardization, and by g-estimation, and explain how they differ.
4. Use propensity scores correctly and check overlap and balance.
5. Use instrumental variables and state what they estimate and the assumptions behind them.
6. Analyse time-varying treatments with g-methods and design a target trial emulation.

## Texts
- **Required:** Hernán and Robins, *Causal Inference: What If* (CRC, 2020; free from the authors): Part I (Chapters 1–10), Part II (Chapters 11–16), and Part III Chapters 19–22.
- **Recommended:** Imbens and Rubin, *Causal Inference for Statistics, Social, and Biomedical Sciences* (Cambridge, 2015; to buy): the parts on classical randomized experiments and on design with unconfounded assignment.
