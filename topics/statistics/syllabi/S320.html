Course description

How to run experiments that people can trust. The first half is classical design of experiments: randomization, blocking, factorial designs. The second half is online A/B testing as large tech companies practise it, from Kohavi, Tang and Xu's book. Topics include choosing metrics, sample ratio mismatch, A/A tests, variance reduction, interference between groups, and long-term effects. This is the course closest to the work you'll do as a data or ML engineer. It ends with a complete experiment on real or realistic logs.

Learning objectives

By the end of this course you can:

  1. Design a randomized experiment with blocking or a factorial structure, and analyse it with ANOVA.
  2. Choose an overall evaluation criterion and guardrail metrics for a product change, and defend the choice.
  3. Compute the sample size and run time for an A/B test, including when the randomization unit differs from the analysis unit.
  4. Detect sample ratio mismatch and other trust failures, and explain Twyman's law.
  5. Apply variance reduction (CUPED) and estimate how much it shortens a test.
  6. Recognize interference, novelty and primacy effects, and design around them.
  7. Write an experiment readout that a product team can act on.

Texts

  • Required: Kohavi, Tang and Xu, Trustworthy Online Controlled Experiments (Cambridge, 2020; to buy): Chapters 1–3, 7, 14, 17–19, 21–23.
  • Required: Montgomery, Design and Analysis of Experiments (Wiley; any recent edition; to buy): chapters on single-factor experiments, randomized blocks, and 2ᵏ factorial designs.
  • Recommended: Bruce, Bruce and Gedeck, Practical Statistics for Data Scientists (in your library), Chapter 3.
