## Course description
Regression as you'll actually use it. Casella and Berger give you the theory of one predictor. This course goes to many
predictors, categorical variables, interactions, diagnostics, and then generalized linear models for yes/no and count outcomes.
The work is done on real data in Python (statsmodels). Each week ends with a short written interpretation, because a regression
nobody can read is no use to anyone.

There is a short pretest on multiple regression. If you already know a topic, we skip it.

## Learning objectives
By the end of this course you can:
1. Fit a multiple regression and interpret each coefficient, including dummy variables and interactions, in plain words.
2. Check a fit with residual plots and leverage and influence measures, and say what to do about each problem you find.
3. Derive least squares in matrix form and the variance of the coefficient vector.
4. Explain the three parts of a generalized linear model (distribution, link, linear predictor) and why the exponential family makes fitting by iteratively reweighted least squares work.
5. Fit and interpret logistic and Poisson regressions, including deviance, overdispersion and odds ratios.
6. Write up a regression analysis that a non-statistician can follow, with its limits stated.

## Texts
- **Required:** Agresti, *Foundations of Linear and Generalized Linear Models* (Wiley, 2015; to buy): Chapter 1 "Introduction to Linear and Generalized Linear Models", Chapter 2 "Linear Models: Least Squares Theory", Chapter 4 "Generalized Linear Models: Model Fitting and Inference", Chapter 5 "Models for Binary Data", Chapter 7 "Models for Count Data".
- **Required:** James, Witten, Hastie and Tibshirani, *An Introduction to Statistical Learning*, 2nd ed. (free from the authors): Chapter 3 "Linear Regression" and §4.3 "Logistic Regression", for the applied side and the Python labs.
- **Software:** Python with pandas and statsmodels.
