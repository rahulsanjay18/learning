Course description

Chapter 6 of Casella and Berger. It's short, but it has more proofs than any chapter so far. The question is how much of a sample you can summarize without losing information about the parameter. We cover sufficient, minimal sufficient, ancillary and complete statistics; Basu's theorem; the likelihood function; the likelihood principle and Birnbaum's argument for it; and the equivariance principle. Most of what comes later in estimation and testing is built on these ideas.

Learning objectives

By the end of this course you can:

  1. Prove the factorization theorem in the discrete case and use it to find a sufficient statistic.
  2. Find a minimal sufficient statistic using the likelihood-ratio criterion (Theorem 6.2.13).
  3. Recognize ancillary statistics and prove ancillarity in location and scale families.
  4. Prove that a statistic is complete in an exponential family, and use Basu's theorem.
  5. State the sufficiency, conditionality and likelihood principles, and explain Birnbaum's argument in your own words.
  6. Explain the equivariance principle with a location example.

Texts

  • Required: Casella and Berger, 2nd ed., Chapter 6, "Principles of Data Reduction", pp. 271–300. In your library.
  • Optional: Jaynes, Probability Theory (in your library), for a Bayesian view of the likelihood principle. Mayo, Statistical Inference as Severe Testing (to buy later), for the main critique of Birnbaum's argument.
