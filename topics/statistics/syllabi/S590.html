Course description

An optional graduate seminar on what statistical inference is for. We read the main positions as arguments: Jaynes's objective Bayesianism, Savage's subjective Bayesianism, and Mayo's severe-testing account of frequentist inference. Each week you write a short response paper and we argue about it.

Learning objectives

By the end of this course you can:

  1. State the strongest version of each position and its main weakness.
  2. Explain the debate over the likelihood principle and Birnbaum's argument.
  3. Defend your own view of when to use Bayesian or frequentist methods, with reasons that would persuade someone who disagrees.

Texts

  • Required: Jaynes, Probability Theory: The Logic of Science (Cambridge, 2003; in your library), Chapters 1–2. Savage, The Foundations of Statistics (Dover, 1972; to buy), Chapters 1–5. Mayo, Statistical Inference as Severe Testing (Cambridge, 2018; to buy), Excursions 1–3.
