## Course description
Graduate probability on a measure-theoretic footing, from the first three chapters of Durrett's *Probability: Theory and
Examples*. We cover measure and integration, independence, the Borel–Cantelli lemmas, the weak and strong laws of large numbers,
characteristic functions, and the central limit theorem with its Lindeberg–Feller generalization. This is the probability
covered on a first-year PhD qualifying exam.

## Learning objectives
By the end of this course you can:
1. Use the monotone convergence, Fatou and dominated convergence theorems, and choose the right one for a given limit.
2. Prove both Borel–Cantelli lemmas and use them to show almost sure convergence.
3. Prove the weak law of large numbers under finite variance, and the strong law (in Etemadi's form).
4. Work with characteristic functions: inversion, the continuity theorem, and moments.
5. Prove the central limit theorem with characteristic functions, and state and apply Lindeberg–Feller.

## Texts
- **Required:** Durrett, *Probability: Theory and Examples*, 5th ed. (Cambridge, 2019; free PDF from the author): Chapter 1 "Measure Theory", Chapter 2 "Laws of Large Numbers", Chapter 3 "Central Limit Theorems".
- **Recommended:** Fremlin, *Measure Theory*, Volume 2 (in your library), for full proofs Durrett sketches.
