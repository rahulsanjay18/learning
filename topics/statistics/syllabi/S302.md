## Course description
Chapter 7 of Casella and Berger: point estimation. The first half covers ways to find an estimator: the method of moments,
maximum likelihood, Bayes estimators, and the EM algorithm. The second half covers how to judge one: mean squared error, unbiased
estimators with the smallest variance, the Cramér–Rao lower bound, the Rao–Blackwell and Lehmann–Scheffé theorems, and loss
functions. This is where the sufficiency results from S301 start to pay off.

## Learning objectives
By the end of this course you can:
1. Find method-of-moments and maximum likelihood estimators, including cases where the likelihood has no interior maximum.
2. Prove the invariance property of maximum likelihood estimators.
3. Compute a Bayes estimator under squared-error loss with a conjugate prior.
4. Explain each step of the EM algorithm and prove that it never decreases the likelihood.
5. Compare estimators by mean squared error and explain the bias–variance tradeoff with an example.
6. Prove the Cramér–Rao inequality, stating its regularity conditions, and find an estimator that attains it.
7. Use Rao–Blackwell and Lehmann–Scheffé to construct the best unbiased estimator.

## Texts
- **Required:** Casella and Berger, 2nd ed., Chapter 7, "Point Estimation", pp. 311–355. In your library.
- **Recommended:** Efron and Hastie, *Computer Age Statistical Inference* (free from the authors), Chapter 4 "Fisherian Inference and Maximum Likelihood Estimation", for a modern take on why the MLE matters.
