## Course description
The rest of Gelman et al., *Bayesian Data Analysis*, after the first five chapters you read in S340. It covers model checking and
comparison, how data collection enters the model, decision analysis, Bayesian computation (MCMC, Hamiltonian Monte Carlo,
variational and Laplace approximations), hierarchical regression and GLMs, robust models, missing data, and Gaussian processes
and mixtures. By the end you should be able to build and criticize a real Bayesian model, not only fit one.

## Learning objectives
By the end of this course you can:
1. Check a model with posterior predictive checks and compare models with cross-validation and information criteria.
2. Explain how sampling and missing-data mechanisms enter a Bayesian model, and when they can be ignored.
3. Implement and diagnose Gibbs, Metropolis and Hamiltonian Monte Carlo, and explain how HMC uses gradients.
4. Use variational and Laplace approximations and say what they get wrong.
5. Fit hierarchical linear and generalized linear models and interpret the variance components.
6. Fit a Gaussian process or finite mixture model and assess it.

## Texts
- **Required:** Gelman et al., *Bayesian Data Analysis*, 3rd ed. (free PDF from the authors): Chapters 6–8, 10–12, 13, 14–16, 18, 21–22.
- **Recommended:** Robert and Casella, *Monte Carlo Statistical Methods* (to buy for S570).
- **Software:** PyMC or Stan.
