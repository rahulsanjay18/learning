## Course description
Chapter 12 of Casella and Berger, the last chapter. It covers three regression models that break the simple assumptions of
Chapter 11: regression when x is also measured with error, logistic regression for a yes/no outcome, and robust regression for
data with outliers. Finishing this course means you've finished the book.

## Learning objectives
By the end of this course you can:
1. Explain why ordinary least squares is biased when x is measured with error, and compute the orthogonal least squares and maximum likelihood fits.
2. Write the logistic regression model, interpret its coefficients as log odds ratios, and fit it by maximum likelihood (Newton–Raphson).
3. Fit a robust regression (least absolute deviation or Huber) and compare it with least squares on contaminated data.
4. Say which of the three models fits a given data problem, and why.

## Texts
- **Required:** Casella and Berger, 2nd ed., Chapter 12, "Regression Models", pp. 577–602. In your library.
- **Recommended:** James et al., *An Introduction to Statistical Learning*, 2nd ed. (free from the authors), Chapter 4 "Classification", §4.3 on logistic regression.
