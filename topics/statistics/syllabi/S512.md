## Course description
Graduate hypothesis testing from Lehmann and Romano's *Testing Statistical Hypotheses*. It covers the decision-theoretic setup,
uniformly most powerful tests, unbiased tests and their applications to normal models, invariance, linear hypotheses, multiple
testing and simultaneous inference, and conditional inference. Multiple testing gets extra time because that's where the
practical problems are.

## Learning objectives
By the end of this course you can:
1. Frame a testing problem in decision-theoretic terms and prove the general Neyman–Pearson lemma.
2. Find uniformly most powerful tests for families with monotone likelihood ratio, and explain when none exist.
3. Construct uniformly most powerful unbiased tests in exponential families, including the t-test as a special case.
4. Use invariance to reduce a testing problem, and derive the F-test for a linear hypothesis.
5. Control the familywise error rate and the false discovery rate, and prove that Benjamini–Hochberg controls FDR under independence.
6. Explain conditional inference and when to condition on an ancillary statistic.

## Texts
- **Required:** Lehmann and Romano, *Testing Statistical Hypotheses*, 3rd ed. (Springer, 2005) or 4th ed. (2022) (to buy): "The General Decision Problem", "Uniformly Most Powerful Tests", "Unbiasedness: Theory and First Applications", "Unbiasedness: Applications to Normal Distributions", "Invariance", "Linear Hypotheses", "Multiple Testing and Simultaneous Inference", "Conditional Inference".
- **Recommended:** Efron and Hastie, *Computer Age Statistical Inference*, Chapter 15, "Large-Scale Hypothesis Testing and FDRs".
