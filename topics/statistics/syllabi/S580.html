Course description

An optional graduate elective on sequential decisions: multi-armed bandits and adaptive experiments. It covers the explore–exploit tradeoff, upper confidence bound algorithms, Thompson sampling, adversarial bandits, regret lower bounds, and contextual and linear bandits. This connects to A/B testing (adaptive allocation instead of fixed splits) and to reinforcement learning, which your 3D chess agent uses.

Learning objectives

By the end of this course you can:

  1. Define regret and prove a regret bound for explore-then-commit.
  2. Prove a logarithmic regret bound for UCB.
  3. Explain and implement Exp3 for adversarial bandits.
  4. Prove the Lai–Robbins style lower bound in outline and explain what it means for algorithm design.
  5. Implement LinUCB and Thompson sampling for a contextual bandit.
  6. Say when an adaptive experiment is better than a fixed A/B split, and what it costs in inference.

Texts

  • Required: Lattimore and Szepesvári, Bandit Algorithms (Cambridge, 2020; free PDF from the authors): Chapter 1 "Introduction", Chapter 4 "Stochastic Bandits", Chapter 6 "The Explore-Then-Commit Algorithm", Chapter 7 "The Upper Confidence Bound Algorithm", Chapter 11 "The Exp3 Algorithm", Chapter 15 "Minimax Lower Bounds", Chapter 16 "Instance-Dependent Lower Bounds", Chapter 19 "Stochastic Linear Bandits", Chapter 36 "Thompson Sampling".
