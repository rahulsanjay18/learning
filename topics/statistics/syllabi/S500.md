## Course description
The first Level II course, and a short one. It reviews the real analysis that graduate probability assumes: sequences and series,
the topology of the real line, continuity, uniform convergence, and the Riemann integral and where it falls short. Then it
introduces measure. You did this in your math degree, so the course starts with a pretest and teaches only what has faded.

## Learning objectives
By the end of this course you can:
1. Prove standard results about limits, continuity and uniform convergence with epsilon–delta arguments.
2. Use compactness and completeness of the real line in proofs.
3. Say exactly when you may swap a limit with an integral under the Riemann integral, and why that motivates Lebesgue's.
4. Define a sigma-algebra, a measure and a measurable function, and construct Lebesgue measure in outline.

## Texts
- **Required:** Abbott, *Understanding Analysis*, 2nd ed. (Springer, 2015; in your library): Chapter 2 "Sequences and Series", Chapter 3 "Basic Topology of R", Chapter 6 "Sequences and Series of Functions", Chapter 7 "The Riemann Integral".
- **Recommended:** Fremlin, *Measure Theory*, Volume 1, "The Irreducible Minimum" (in your library), opening chapters on measure spaces and Lebesgue measure.
