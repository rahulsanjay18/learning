## Course description
An optional course on forecasting from Hyndman and Athanasopoulos's *Forecasting: Principles and Practice*, the standard free
textbook. We cover graphics and decomposition, simple benchmark methods, exponential smoothing, ARIMA, regression with time
series errors, and how to evaluate a forecast honestly. The work uses real series, and every model is compared against a naive
benchmark first.

## Learning objectives
By the end of this course you can:
1. Plot and decompose a time series, and describe its trend, seasonality and autocorrelation.
2. Set naive and seasonal-naive benchmarks and evaluate forecasts on a held-out test set and with time-series cross-validation.
3. Fit and choose exponential smoothing models.
4. Identify, fit and check ARIMA models, including seasonal ones.
5. Fit a regression with ARIMA errors and forecast with known future predictors.
6. Produce prediction intervals and explain why they are usually too narrow.

## Texts
- **Required:** Hyndman and Athanasopoulos, *Forecasting: Principles and Practice*, 3rd ed. (free online at otexts.com/fpp3): Chapters 2 "Time series graphics", 3 "Time series decomposition", 5 "The forecaster's toolbox", 8 "Exponential smoothing", 9 "ARIMA models", 10 "Dynamic regression models".
- **Recommended:** the 2nd edition in your library has the same material with older R code.
- **Software:** the book's examples are in R. You can do the course in R, or in Python with statsmodels or statsforecast.
